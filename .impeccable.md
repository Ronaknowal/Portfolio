## Design Context

### Users
The learning hub serves a broad technical audience: complete beginners learning fundamentals, experienced professionals switching into AI/ML, and practitioners or researchers who need a reliable place to learn new material or systematically revise a field. Visitors need to find an appropriate starting point quickly, understand prerequisites, and make steady progress through a very large curriculum.

### Brand Personality
Technically fluent, encouraging, and intellectually serious. The Learn experience extends the portfolio's "training loop" personality: knowledge is distilled, progress is visible, and the interface rewards curiosity without making newcomers feel excluded.

### Aesthetic Direction
Keep the existing dark, near-black training-dashboard aesthetic, with amber as the primary identity accent and green for progress. Avoid generic course-marketplace cards and loud AI-neon treatments. The content hierarchy should feel calm and editorial even when the underlying subject matter is dense.

### Design Principles
1. Orient learners before presenting complexity: paths, goals, prerequisites, and next steps come before exhaustive catalogues.
2. Make curriculum scale legible: group related material, disclose depth progressively, and keep universal search and revision routes close at hand.
3. Be honest about content readiness: distinguish published lessons from planned material while keeping the decided teaching sequence. Planned entries show their outline and actual adjacent topics; publication must not cause a shortcut past them.
4. Support both guided and non-linear study: a beginner can follow a sequence while an expert can jump directly to a precise topic.
5. Preserve the training-loop visual language while maintaining readable contrast, keyboard access, and responsive navigation in the dark-only interface.
