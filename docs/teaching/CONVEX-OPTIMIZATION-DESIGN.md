## Implemented core progression and practical scope

Begin with a closest-feasible resource plan. Distinguish a point, a segment, an objective's graph and a feasible region before technical terms. Develop convex sets/functions and prove the local-to-global result. State existence and uniqueness separately. Derive supporting-plane and constrained first-order certificates; a nonzero gradient at a boundary is expected. Build common objectives with valid composition and epigraph rules. Use ridge to connect Hessians, strong convexity, conditioning, step sizes and changed statistical assumptions. Derive subgradients and a scalar proximal operator, then show how a smooth step combines with projection/shrinkage. Close with a checked complete modeling workflow, original practical applications and independent counterexamples/transfer.

Useful applications to assess include actuator allocation with a shared limit, regression under correlated features, robust worst-case fit via an epigraph, and one-dimensional denoising/sparsity. Distinguish a pedagogical invented system from a documented deployed application. Do not promise every convex problem is computationally easy or every nonconvex problem hard. Convexity does not certify the data or generalization.
