## Finish line and teaching boundaries

A learner should be able to take a simple transport, thermal or vibrating-medium story and write its domain, units, equation, initial and boundary data; derive a representative solution; check the equation and all data; explain information flow, conserved/dissipated quantities and nonuniqueness or instability; then supply a valid target to a numerical solver. This is a usable introductory PDE course within one organized long lesson, not a catalogue of equation names or a promise to teach every PDE theorem.

The first pass uses real scalar fields on a line, a finite interval and a square. Define each added spatial dimension, integration measure and physical quantity. Core derivations cover transport, heat, wave, Poisson, compatibility and energy. Deeper sections develop the exact weak point-source example, a bounded Burgers Riemann problem, inverse heat sensitivity and periodic penetration. These examples earn their place by explaining where classical formulas or naive inference fail. General Sobolev theory, all nonlinear existence/regularity, Navier–Stokes, numerical schemes, general spectral theory and machine-learned PDE solvers remain clearly identified further study. No important data requirement or sign convention is hidden in an optional disclosure.

## Learning route and local derivations

1. **A field is many linked local observations.** Start with a thin rod whose temperature differs by location. Define `u(x,t)`, a snapshot at fixed t and a sensor trace at fixed x. A time derivative does not describe spatial slope. Use temperature departure in kelvin, x in metres, t in seconds, volumetric capacity C in J/(m³ K), conductivity k in W/(m K), source s in W/m³ and signed axial heat flux j in W/m². Work per unit cross-sectional area. For constant C>0 and positive conductivity k, show `C d/dt ∫[a,b]u dx = j(a,t)−j(b,t)+∫[a,b]s dx`, then the FTC/continuity argument gives `C u_t+j_x=s`. Distinguish an amount balance from a temperature formula. A generic transported density uses its own explicitly declared units.

2. **Spatial operators measure local changes, not new mysterious symbols.** Derive the rectangular flux identity by adding opposite-face flows, dividing by area and applying the one-variable FTC in each direction. Define `∇u`, `∇·j`, `Δu` and the outward normal. Show `j=−k∇u` as a constitutive assumption, then `C u_t=∇·(k∇u)+s`. If k varies, the one-dimensional result is `k u_xx+k_x u_x`; replacing it by `kΔu` loses a term. Give a complete polynomial verification. General smooth-domain divergence theorem is a named extension with regularity assumptions, not a theorem proved by drawing a rectangle.

3. **An equation is only one part of a problem.** Introduce domain, time horizon, initial condition and boundary condition with a space-time strip. For an interval, outward normals are −1 at the left and +1 at the right. Fixed value is Dirichlet; fixed outward flux is Neumann; heat transfer to a bath is Robin: `−k∂_n u=h(u−u_env)`, equivalently `k∂_n u+h(u−u_env)=0`, h≥0. Periodic endpoints must match both the field and the relevant flux; they identify locations rather than independently prescribe two wall temperatures. Explain compatible corner data, and distinguish continuity at a corner from stronger derivative compatibility. Define well-posedness as existence, uniqueness and continuous dependence in declared data/solution classes and norms. Never infer all three from a plotted candidate. First-order inflow and second-order wave/heat data requirements will be derived separately.

4. **Transport carries information along paths.** For constant positive v, derive `d/dt u(x₀+vt,t)=u_t+v u_x=0`. Trace a point backward to t=0 or to the left inflow boundary, whichever it reaches first. The right endpoint is outflow, not independent input. Solve a piecewise initial/inflow example, check both PDE branches and their corner match, and derive its integral balance. For variable velocity, conservative `u_t+(vu)_x=0` differs from `u_t+v u_x=0`; the additional compression/expansion term is local and will be explicitly explained. No all-purpose arbitrary-characteristic solver is promised.

5. **Diffusion changes shape by damping spatial modes.** Derive heat from flux, then introduce `u=X(x)T(t)` as a search for special solutions rather than a claim every solution is one product. On regions where division is legal, the independent variables force the separated expression to be a constant; the resulting ODEs extend through zeros. Set `−X''=λX`, `T'=−αλT`. Check negative, zero and positive λ and impose endpoint conditions. Dirichlet selects sine modes with n≥1; Neumann selects cosines including n=0; a mixed-end changed exercise selects half-integer modes. Define the nonzero eigenfunction and distinguish it from zero solution. Show coefficient projection by actual integration and explain superposition under linear homogeneous conditions. For nonhomogeneous boundaries first subtract a compatible lifting/steady solution; do not superpose incompatible boundary values.

6. **The same initial heat can leave or remain.** Compare `f(ξ)=sin²(πξ)` on 0≤ξ≤1 under zero-value versus insulated boundaries; use dimensionless time `θ=αt/L²`, explicitly related to the physical rod. Show the exact Neumann finite formula and derived Dirichlet coefficients. Explain constant-mode survival, mean heat versus the squared-temperature norm, and why energy terminology must name its quantity. The initial function matches both endpoint values and slopes, but higher parabolic corner compatibility differs; claim smoothness for t>0 and continuous initial attainment, not a C²,¹ extension through every corner. Establish the absolute coefficient bound and positive-time convergence of differentiated series locally. A finite plotted sum has a truncation bound plus separate floating error; zero machine underflow is not a zero analytic tail.

7. **What can be proved without solving every coefficient?** Multiply the homogeneous heat equation by u and integrate by parts. For homogeneous Dirichlet/Neumann or dissipative Robin boundaries, derive `½C d/dt∫u² = −k∫u_x² −h₀u(0)²−h_Lu(L)²` with terms appropriate to the selected boundary. Apply it to the difference of two solutions with the same source/boundary data for uniqueness and initial-data L² stability. Explain when constant differences survive for steady Neumann problems. Give a local weak maximum-principle proof using a strict perturbation and the parabolic boundary (initial/base and spatial sides, not final time as prescribed data). State C² in space, C¹ in time in the interior and continuity on the closed cylinder. Then introduce the normalized whole-line heat kernel, verify its PDE, mass and variance, and prove the initial approximate-identity limit for bounded uniformly continuous f. Whole-line uniqueness needs a bounded/growth class; do not silently omit it. Nonnegative nonzero compact heat spreads instantly in this ideal diffusion model; contrast the later finite-speed wave, not physical relativity.

8. **Waves need a shape and a velocity.** Derive `ρ u_tt=T u_xx` from small-slope string force balance, with c²=T/ρ and units. Change to characteristic coordinates to derive d'Alembert's two-traveling-part form; integrate the initial velocity to obtain the full formula. Identify the initial interval `[x−ct,x+ct]` determining an observation. Use a compact C² displacement and optional nonzero velocity, rather than falsely declaring Gaussian tails compact. Derive the fixed-end sine standing modes and separate each amplitude's cosine/sine time constants. The energy `½∫(u_t²+c²u_x²)` has zero derivative for fixed or free homogeneous ends under the stated regularity; it moves between kinetic and strain terms. Odd fixed-end and even free-end reflection are concise geometric extensions, not an invented need for Abstract Algebra55.

9. **A steady field transmits boundary information through space.** Derive `−∇·(k∇u)=s` from the time-independent balance. Work the one-dimensional forced Poisson polynomial with independently checked endpoint fluxes. Then use a genuine square problem with top boundary `sin(nπx)` and other edges zero: derive `sin(nπx)sinh(nπy)/sinh(nπ)` and verify its Laplacian. Show how finer boundary variation fades with depth. Derive uniqueness for fixed Dirichlet data by the energy or harmonic maximum argument; “at most one” is not existence for arbitrary rough domains/data. For Neumann, integrate the balance to obtain source/outflow compatibility, exhibit the additive constant family and fix a mean when appropriate. Source=2 with two zero outward fluxes has no steady solution; source=2 with outward fluxes1/1 has a family. Keep sign convention visible.

10. **A weak formulation preserves balance when classical derivatives fail.** Explain a test function as a smooth probe and move derivatives by integration by parts. On [0,1], a unit point source at a gives `G(x,a)=min(x,a)(1−max(x,a))`: continuous, slope jump −1, no ordinary second derivative at a. Derive `∫G'v'=v(a)` by integrating on the two smooth pieces. Define the one-dimensional zero-boundary energy space locally as absolutely continuous functions with square-integrable derivative, and explain point evaluation is bounded using the FTC and Cauchy–Schwarz. Derive the minimization functional, its first variation and the exact nonnegative energy difference; this is the bridge needed by57, not a substitute for general Sobolev/distribution theory. Include the Neumann compatibility interpretation with a constant test function. Distinguish weak derivatives from weak convergence.

11. **Nonlinear conservation can form a shock.** For Burgers, derive characteristic speeds equal to the transported value and show the denominator in `u_x=u₀'/(1+t u₀')`. A simple smooth initial profile explains loss of classical regularity. Derive Rankine–Hugoniot from a moving control volume. Two reversed Riemann states can obey the same jump speed while only the compressive shock is entropy-admissible. Develop the bounded convex-flux shock/rarefaction example, including a quadratic entropy pair, rather than use “weak solution” as permission for any discontinuity. State the full family of convex entropy inequalities when describing the broader admissibility class; the one quadratic test is a worked diagnostic for this example, not a general uniqueness proof for arbitrary nonlinear systems or nonconvex fluxes.

12. **Useful consequences, capstone and next step.** A high-frequency unit-amplitude heat mode is exponentially tiny at positive T; this sequence proves discontinuity of the backwards map in the stated norm, independently of a finite graph. Explain why numerical instability and ill-posed input recovery are different problems. Derive the periodic half-line penetration depth and distinguish periodic steady response from startup. Finish with a fully specified forced rod, a uniform settling criterion and an independent changed case. Close with exact Numerical PDE57 as the next module topic; prepare equation/data/norm/known-solution/verification targets without skipping to another published lesson.

The regular Sturm–Liouville connection is optional depth adjacent to modes: define `−(pX')'+qX=λwX` with p>0, w>0, p continuously differentiable, q/w continuous and real on a finite closed interval, and real homogeneous separated endpoint conditions with nonzero endpoint coefficient pairs. Use the weighted inner product and the actual boundary form to prove distinct-eigenvalue orthogonality. Nonnegative eigenvalues require the extra nonnegative potential and dissipative endpoint assumptions; the differential expression alone does not specify a self-adjoint operator. The general completeness theorem is a qualified further result; core sine/cosine completeness uses the already reviewed Fourier route. State the chosen convention explicitly because the reference's use of “regular” additionally includes nonnegativity, which is not universal terminology.

Classification is a compact synthesis **after** the mechanisms: for `A u_xx+2B u_xy+C u_yy+lower terms`, use `B²−AC` with the 2B convention named. Explain the principal quadratic form and local coefficients. A zero discriminant alone does not ensure a useful evolution equation or well-posed problem; boundary type, lower-order terms and function class still matter. Do not let three names replace the derivations above.

## Practice and observable acceptance

Practice is interleaved, then a changed capstone combines taught steps. Each task needs an optional hint, an explained solution, explicit assumptions and why the tempting wrong answer fails. Sample acceptance criteria to retain and verify during authoring:

| Changed task | Evidence of understanding |
| --- | --- |
| Supply k(x)=1+x and a polynomial temperature; repair a missing kₓuₓ term | Derive flux first, then independently verify the resulting source and total balance. |
| Interpret a left endpoint reporting outward heat loss | Use n=−1; do not copy the right endpoint derivative sign. Explain Robin bath exchange and units. |
| Change an inflow after a specified time | Trace the actual observation backward and decide whether the change can affect it. No arbitrary endpoint data at outflow. |
| Change one end from fixed value to zero flux | Derive λ and half-integer sine/cosine modes from both endpoint equations; distinguish zero solution from a valid eigenfunction. |

| Start a string flat but with nonzero velocity | Use the velocity integral and verify both initial data; two translated initial shapes alone give a wrong zero answer. |
| Prescribe a nonzero source with insulated steady boundaries | Show the integral contradiction; a mean constraint cannot repair missing compatibility. |
| Change the point-source location and test probe | Integrate on both pieces, recover the source weight, and explain why classical second derivatives are the wrong language at the kink. |
| Reverse shock states | Check jump speed and entropy sign, then construct the rarefaction in its three regions. State its t>0 scope and initial trace. |
| Compare forward heat prediction and recovering its initial mode | Provide a sequence/norm argument for the inverse failure; distinguish it from a time discretization failing its stability condition. |

| Hand a model to Numerical PDE57 | Specify the continuous unknown, domain, data, reference solution, norm/quantity, compatibility and known analytic identity to test. A small discrete residual is not the acceptance criterion by itself. |

It does require explaining the representation, deriving/checking the representative solution and diagnosing a changed data or assumption failure without copying the exact worked input.
