## First-pass learning sequence

Use an invented small catalogue of seven workshops, users A–E and the preserved 20 observed 1–5 ratings. The exact old numerical matrix is retained but all outputs are recomputed. Matrix examples used for fitting all known ratings teach mechanisms only; their training fit is never called a held-out result. The evaluation workflow introduces an explicitly separate timestamped event fixture and its release/eligibility table, with all records shown and split before aggregation. Explain the dataset change at that point.

1. **Choose an item under an information boundary.** Start with the actual decision: return up to K available, eligible items for this request. Distinguish user, item, interaction, request, displayed impression, rating and target outcome. A missing rating, a recorded zero action and an explicit negative rating are different. Define what happens for a new user/item, fewer eligible items, ties and repeat recommendations.
2. **Freeze the experiment before learning.** Show a global cutoff and train/validation/test time windows. Learn counts, means, similarities, factors, hyperparameters and feature transforms only from allowed records. Availability at the request time and a permitted-history rule define candidates. Warm-user versus cold-user evaluation asks different questions. Define a popularity baseline and explicit mean/shrunk-item baseline; explain why their weakness or success is evidence, not proof of absent personal signal.
3. **Borrow evidence from neighbors.** Trace the common-rating set, raw cosine, user-centering and weighted residual prediction. Derive why one common positive rating gives cosine 1 without establishing reliability. Show minimum support, n/(n+shrinkage), normalization, negative similarities and deterministic fallback. Missing coordinates are not filled zeros merely to compute a dense cosine. Centering conventions must match both similarity and prediction.
4. **Compress a pattern into factors.** Expand one dot product term by term, with matrices U(users×d), V(items×d), and UVᵀ. Add global/user/item biases before interpreting factors. Distinguish parameter sharing from recovering true preference. Exhibit two different rank-one completions with identical observed diagonal entries; low rank alone does not identify missing values. Orthogonal rotations preserve scores and L2 penalty, so factor axes need not be semantic genres.
5. **Train the model that the objective actually defines.** Use local loss ½[e²+λ(||p||²+||q||²+bᵤ²+bᵢ²)] and explicitly sum it over observed records for the full SGD trainer. Thus regularization repeats per observation; compare with the once-per-entity penalty as a different convention, not the same formula. Derive gradients, use old p/q for both simultaneous updates, distinguish one-pair loss, epoch-end objective, rolling online error and held-out error. A large step may raise loss; zero vectors can leave the bilinear part stuck. Recompute epoch-end diagnostics after the epoch and save the best validation choice without consulting test labels.
6. **Solve one factor block exactly.** For a once-per-entity regularized observed-only objective, fixed item factors give (QΩᵀQΩ+λI)p=QΩᵀr; subtract a separately declared fixed bias if used. Derive the equation and uniqueness for λ>0. Include the option λ·degree for the frequency-penalized counterpart, and explain which the program uses. Exact coordinate steps do not increase that objective; bounded monotone objective values alone are not a global-optimum or unique-factor theorem. Use solves, not an explicit inverse.
7. **Change feedback, change the loss.** For counts n, define binary target z=1[n>0] and confidence c=1+αn. This is not a click-probability model. Expand one user's all-item quadratic into the cached YᵀY plus observed corrections and the right-hand side. Show how α, λ and a missing-item factor alter the optimum, including empty history p=0. Derive the additional O(d³) direct solve cost and storage; the cache is recomputed when the opposite factors change. Preserve the original dense miniature as an intelligible reference, followed by a sparse-equivalent implementation.
8. **Train a comparison instead of a rating.** A BPR pair assumes observed item i should outrank sampled unobserved j for u. Let Δ=s(u,i)−s(u,j); derive −logσ(Δ)=softplus(−Δ), gradient −σ(−Δ), and the three factor updates from old values. Compare pairwise surrogate loss to actual ranking metrics; user/global bias cancels in Δ. Show a hard versus easy pair and a false-negative caveat. Sampling defines the optimized empirical weighting; it is not free evidence of dislike.
9. **Evaluate the list you actually serve.** Define precision, recall, average precision, reciprocal rank and binary/graded NDCG with ranks starting at one, ideal denominator, score-tie policy, and users with no relevant eligible test item reported separately. Compute a concrete list by hand. Show a missed relevant candidate cannot be repaired by reranking, and how removing unseen negatives artificially inflates evaluation. Explain observed relevance limitations, warm/cold cohorts, user versus interaction averaging and uncertainty at the user/session sampling unit.
10. **Use current libraries without changing the question.** Give complete CPU examples for implicit 0.7.3 and Surprise 1.1.5, shape checks and true outputs. Use `num_threads=1`, fixed RNG and explicit solver/settings for inspectability; reproducibility is a tested snapshot, not bitwise portability promise. Construct confidence only for stored entries, fit user×item and pass the matching row to recommend. Filter only after clearly deciding whether historical positives are ineligible. Verify cold-ID fallback rather than relying on an arbitrary learned factor.
11. **Add content and retrieve candidates.** Derive a simple metadata-to-factor ridge map from warm items and score a held-out new item's features; it transfers learned associations and can fail out of distribution. Include a small catalogue-retrieval/rerank pipeline figure. Exact scoring costs O(items·d), biases may be included with an augmented dot product, cosine normalization changes the objective, and approximate indexes introduce measurable recall/latency tradeoffs rather than a universal logarithmic bound. Describe sequential/two-tower context as a bridge with clear timestamps and owners.
12. **Separate clicks, exposure and intervention.** Distinguish observed interactions from the action that produced exposure. Give a finite two-item known-randomized logging policy and derive the importance ratio for a target policy. Show the expected-value identity, a complete finite logged sample, variance and loss of identifiability at zero logging support. Estimated/incorrect propensities and omitted confounding can invalidate the interpretation; clipping trades bias/variance. Link to Causal Inference/Decision Theory for deeper foundations. Diversity/novelty constraints and feedback concerns are stated objectives/risks, not proof every recommender traps users.
13. **Build and diagnose a changed report.** Supply complete timestamped records, train/validation/test cutoffs, released items, a frozen popularity baseline, explicit MF comparison, rating error and a declared ranking relevance rule. Tune only on validation; publish actual small-sample results and cohort sizes. The learner changes the cutoff or cold-item availability, reruns, and explains changed candidate sets and failure cases. The report is pedagogical, not a deployed-system benchmark.
14. **Independent practice and next steps.** Interleave small predictions earlier; end with changed calculations, implementation repair and a combined report. Separate optional hints from explanations. Bridge one scored user-item pair to several simultaneous target outputs in the actual next lesson.

## Native programs and independent practice contracts

The native route uses Python3 and NumPy/SciPy, with two clearly optional pinned library programs. Each displayed program is standalone: no hidden earlier variables, downloads, credentials or unavailable MovieLens file. Programs include preserved-matrix audit, baseline/split, neighborhood CF, explicit factor training, exact observed ALS, implicit dense/sparse equivalence, BPR step/training, ranking evaluator, content ridge/cold item, importance-policy evaluation, library runs and combined changed report. Final count follows complete workflows, not a quota. Stored questions must render before their code through a topic-owned wrapper, not merely exist in data.

Acceptance cases include an all-missing user, item with no training support, singleton overlap, zero-variance centered vector, empty eligible list, fewer than K candidates, duplicate recommendation rejection, negative implicit score, zero propensity and no-relevant test user. The UI accepts only bounded supported states; invalid drafts must explain and preserve the last valid state. Signed similarity and zero-valued observed ratings must not be conflated with missing values.

Changed practice will require: (a) identify leakage in a time/availability fixture, (b) recompute a centered prediction with support/shrinkage and explain fallback, (c) construct two compatible low-rank completions, (d) derive/test a changed simultaneous SGD update and repair use of new p, (e) reconcile per-record versus per-entity penalties, (f) solve a changed weighted ALS block including a zero-history case, (g) calculate a BPR gap/update with changed sampler, (h) calculate and compare full/sampled-candidate metrics including no relevant items, (i) explain a new-item metadata prediction and its limitations, (j) evaluate a changed logging policy/support counterexample, and (k) produce a revised complete experiment report. Every substantial task has a hint and separately hidden explained solution; numeric/code tasks have executed changed inputs and concrete accepted output. Open decisions have criteria and an example reasoned answer, not fabricated unique answers.

Independent author oracles will use dense NumPy/SciPy solves versus JS closed forms, finite differences or Torch autodiff for gradient signs/old-value updates, exact small masked sums, exhaustive rank permutations, independently enumerated policy expectations, and fixed item/user mappings for library behavior. Fixed seeds control repeated miniature experiments; uncertainty and held-out claims are kept distinct from training traces. Original source/code conservation and repair disposition are checked explicitly rather than silently deleting incorrect-but-useful workflows.
