## Teaching route, complete examples and practice evidence

Use an original declared motion model: position s(t)=t³−6t²+9t metres with t measured in seconds on[0,4]. The coefficients carry the units needed for position; equivalently t is its numerical value in the stated unit. Velocity is3(t−1)(t−3)m/s, acceleration6t−12m/s². The particle visits positions0,4,0,4 at times0,1,3,4. Net displacement4m, distance12m. This is an instructional model, not measured vehicle data.

1. **Three questions about one changing quantity.** Locate position, an interval's average velocity and its total change. A motion track and aligned position/velocity curves introduce negative direction and units before symbols. Complete secant arithmetic at t=2 for h=1 and h=1/2; prediction at a turning point. Do not call signed velocity speed.
2. **A limit is an enforceable closeness statement.** Start with x² near2, a changed value at exactly2 and a right jump. Distinguish a limit from f(2), one-sided disagreement, divergence and oscillation. Explain limit laws with denominator/domain qualifications. Introduce epsilon as requested output accuracy and delta as an input guarantee, in the correct quantifier order. Prove delta=min(1,epsilon/5) for x²→4. A finite sample table can reveal failure but cannot prove all-nearby behavior. Define continuity and give the IVT/EVT with hypotheses and counterexamples to dropping compactness/continuity.
3. **Derive the derivative, not a tiny division by zero.** Difference quotient for the motion cubic, exact expansion and limit. The derivative gives the linear part with remainder/h→0. Show x²'s exact h² error and |x|'s unequal one-sided slopes; a vertical tangent has no finite derivative. Differentiability implies continuity; the converse fails. A tangent can cross the graph at an inflection point. Introduce higher derivatives and their units.
4. **Build derivatives from local operations.** Prove sum, scalar, product and chain rules by differences or remainder composition, including a zero inner derivative. Reciprocal/quotient with nonzero denominator; integer power via induction and negative powers via reciprocal. A product rectangle identifies the second-order cross area. A chain diagram uses inner3x+1 and outeru² with exact finite changes and predicted changes. Derive sine/cosine using the radian limit sin(h)/h→1 and angle identities, with a geometric squeeze; tangent, inverse-function slope and implicit circle example state their domains. General real powers wait for logarithms below.
5. **Use local rates to make global decisions carefully.** Prove Fermat's interior-extremum condition, Rolle via EVT, and MVT by subtracting the secant line. Derive constant-derivative/monotonicity/error-bound consequences. Solve extrema on a closed interval by endpoints, stationary points and nondifferentiable points; distinguish x³'s stationary inflection and |x−2|'s cusp. Work a fixed-volume open-top box cost or a simpler fixed-perimeter rectangle optimization with boundary interpretation; choose one complete non-redundant application. Related-rate example states what changes with time.
6. **Accumulate signed contributions.** Derive Riemann sums from rate×duration and a partition/tag/mesh definition. Continuous functions on compact intervals are integrable (declared theorem; uniform-continuity sketch available). More panels alone is not the definition if the mesh fails to shrink. Work x² sums using a proved/linked sum-of-squares identity and a changed native exact Fraction calculation. For motion, distinguish signed displacement, total distance and absolute net change. Show positive and negative rectangles on one signed axis. One fixed-time integral is a number; variable endpoint creates a new function.
7. **Prove the bridge in both directions.** For continuous f, A(x)=integral_a^x f has derivative f: bound the average residual on the small added interval, handling negative h. Then any antiderivative differs from A by a constant via MVT, so integral_a^b f=F(b)−F(a). Continuous is a sufficient hypothesis; do not claim every integrable function has an antiderivative. A step's accumulation has a corner at the jump. Derive variable-bound chain rule, reversing bounds, additivity, average value and initial-value constants. Complete independent moving-bound example.
8. **Reverse operations to integrate.** Definite substitution follows chain+FTC with continuous integrand and differentiable substitution, including nonmonotone path cancellation when using the chain-rule form; do not require one-to-one where the theorem does not. Derive integration by parts from product rule, retain boundary terms, and work a genuine example. Partial fractions preserve poles/intervals. Provide a compact method-selection table plus actual worked substitution, parts, simple rational and trigonometric examples. A valid integral may have no elementary antiderivative; defining an accumulation function still works.
9. **Finish exponential growth from first principles.** Define L(x)=integral_1^x dt/t for x>0. FTC gives L'=1/x; difference differentiation or substitution gives the product law. Show strictly increasing, continuous, unbounded in both directions using powers of2, so its continuous inverse exp exists. Prove inverse derivative with reciprocal difference quotients (no assumption of inverse differentiability), hence exp'=exp. Let e=exp(1). The integral bounds 1/(1+1/n)≤nL(1+1/n)≤1 prove(1+1/n)^n→e. Derive exp(kt), b^x, log_b and x^r for x>0. State dimensions and distinguish k from a per-period fraction exp(kΔ)−1. Complete doubling/decay calculation, no empirical growth guarantee.
10. **Approximate with an explicit remainder.** A finite Taylor polynomial matches local derivatives; derive integral remainder by repeated integration by parts under C^(n+1) assumptions, then bound by M|h|^(n+1)/(n+1)!. Recover linear error bound and a changed sqrt example. Plot actual exp and log(1+x) polynomials and errors with honest bounds; degree increases do not universally improve distant approximations. Prove exp's remainder vanishes at every fixed x via eventual ratio bound. Give the log series on |x|<1 using the geometric remainder; endpoints need separate reasoning and do not inherit an open-interval proof. Show a single floating-point derivative cancellation example and link numerical treatment.
11. **Inspect limits at the edge of the domain.** Define an improper integral as a limit of finite integrals. Derive integral_1^infinity x^(−p) convergence iff p>1 and integral_0^1 x^(−p) iff p<1, with logarithmic p=1 boundary. A singular endpoint can have finite area; truncation error must be shown. Split at interior singularities and both infinities independently; symmetric cancellation is only a principal value, not ordinary convergence. Optional finite-endpoint0/0 l'Hôpital proof via Cauchy MVT, with derivative-ratio/domain assumptions; never use it circularly for the trig derivative limit. General variants linked, not asserted without conditions.
12. **Use one idea in several settings.** Motion synthesis, a spatially varying one-dimensional force/work model and a nonuniform material-density/center-of-mass calculation expose different units and weighting. If cross-section volume or arc length is included, derive its slice/segment mechanism and solve a complete changed example rather than list formulas. Keep this branch locally supported; stochastic meanings belong to the next topic. A simple ODE/Euler bridge emphasizes solution versus finite trajectory and its initial value. Choose useful applications, not a fixed catalogue of industries.
13. **Independent practice and next step.** Tasks include changed epsilon proof, repaired endpoint limit, cusp derivative, product/chain diagnosis, interval extrema with endpoints, signed versus total motion, moving-bound FTC, substitution with domain, parts boundary error, instantaneous/discrete exponential rate, Taylor bound and improper-integral/principal-value diagnosis. Each substantial task has a separate hint and reasoned answer. Finish a changed motion or weighted-material capstone with explicit intermediate values and acceptance criteria. Link Random Variables next; explain why weighted sums and integrals will acquire probability meanings there.

These are conceptual stages, not fixed final headings or a word/lab quota. Merge only when the required reasoning stays visible; disclose additional proof depth without hiding the core mechanism.
