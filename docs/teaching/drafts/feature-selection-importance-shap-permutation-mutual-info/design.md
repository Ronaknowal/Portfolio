### Two facts about the saved tree worth recording

The root threshold 12.78000020980835 is a **midpoint between two float32 values and is not itself a float32**, so feeding it back as a measurement casts upward and takes the right child. Node 1's threshold 1.0049999952316284 **is** a float32, so a double just above it rounds back onto it and takes the left child, which a comparison of unconverted doubles would get wrong. Both behaviours are asserted, and the second is the case that makes `Math.fround` load-bearing rather than decorative.

## Disposition of the independent review

The [independent review](../../FEATURE-SELECTION-INDEPENDENT-REVIEW.md) recomputed every stated number from first principles, importing nothing from this lesson's code: a from-scratch refit of the whole Wine campaign from the served bytes, Shapley by exhaustive enumeration over all d! orderings in exact `Fraction` arithmetic, mutual information over 300 random tables at fifty digits, the saved tree node for node against both a native fit and an independent Gini recomputation, and `shap.TreeExplainer` against its own exhaustive oracle across all twelve explained rows. **3,194 checks, zero numerical disagreements.** Both float32 boundary claims were confirmed, including that the root threshold is the arithmetic midpoint between two float32 values. It raised one blocking finding, five to fix and eight observations.

| Finding | What was wrong | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
