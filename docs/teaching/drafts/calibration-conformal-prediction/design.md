### Departures, each with its reason

1. **Two investigations open on fixtures the manuscript does not work through.**
   Investigations 2 and 3 ask for an absolute answer rather than a change, so
   there is no "an edit is required first" to stop a learner reading the opening
   answer straight out of the adjacent prose. Investigation 2 opens on six of its
   own observations and investigation 3 on nine cards whose eighth is .55 rather
   than .6 — which makes the opening candidate's leading score land exactly *on*
   the threshold, so the first question a learner meets turns on whether the
   comparison includes equality, and its answer is nowhere in the text. Both
   manuscript fixtures are one preset away. Investigations 1 and 4 need no such
   change: they compare two states and refuse to grade without an edit.

2. **`controlSteps.score` widened from ±5 to ±10.** The practice-2 preset uses a
   score of 6, which a ±5 control could load but not type. Found by the models
   verifier's control-reachability sweep, not by reading.

3. **The sigmoid slope and offset shown for the frozen eight-score fixture are
   the packet's recorded values, not the browser's own fit.** This is the
   recorded disagreement. The manuscript quotes a≈.2677017731 and
   b≈−.1338508865 at ten decimals, from SciPy's BFGS. An independent damped
   Newton solver written for this implementation returns a=0.26770177303558 and
   b=−0.13385088651779, differing by 5.2×10⁻¹¹ and 3.7×10⁻¹¹; the fitted
   probabilities differ by at most 4.9×10⁻¹¹. Both points are stationary to
   within 10⁻¹⁴ and neither is beaten by a 6,561-point grid around it, so
   **neither is wrong** — they are two optimisers stopping at different last
   digits of the same flat minimum. Displaying the live value would contradict
   the manuscript's tenth decimal, so the frozen value is displayed for the
   frozen fixture and the live solver is used only when a learner edits the
   observations. The verifier asserts the two agree to 10⁻⁸ and records the gap.

4. **The sigmoid solver reports convergence at a gradient below 10⁻⁷, not
   10⁻¹⁰.** Near the optimum this objective is flat to second order, so once the
   gradient reaches about 10⁻⁸ the improvement a step would buy is at the
   relative precision of a double and no strictly decreasing step exists. A
   tighter threshold reported perfectly good fits as failures on some labellings,
   which would have hidden the comparison curve from a learner whose only action
   was editing a label. A `stalled` flag records that the search ran out of
   representable progress rather than reaching its tolerance, and the verifier
   proves every stalled point is still a local minimum by grid.

5. **Three displayed programs, assembled rather than transcribed.** The
   manuscript presents its two programs as downloads and shows no fenced code.
   The teaching standard asks for compact displayed code that shows the
   mechanism, so `reliability_bins.py`, `monotone_map.py` and `conformal_rank.py`
   are served and displayed. Each is built by slicing the frozen
   `calibration_calculations.py` **syntax tree** and emitting the named functions
   verbatim, plus a short driver held in the verifier; the assembled text must
   equal the served asset byte for byte, so neither side can be hand-edited. The
   packet's own guard blocks are retained rather than rewritten: a learner
   editing bin boundaries can plausibly supply a list that does not span the
   probability scale, which is exactly the failure those guards name.

6. **No uncertainty intervals on the reliability diagram.** The visual
   specification defers them explicitly — "no decorative error bars are required
   by this packet" — and adding them would require specifying and verifying an
   interval method and its sampling assumptions. The count rail, the printed
   denominators and the empty-bin rows carry the finite-bin message instead.

7. **Class absence is handled, which the manuscript does not treat.** The Naive
   Bayes destination note asked for "how class absence/small calibration samples
   alter interpretation"; the manuscript covers small samples but not absence.
   `fitSigmoid` therefore returns a stated no-value answer rather than a number
   when a calibration sample carries one outcome class, investigation 1 can grade
   AUC and reports that it has no value on a one-class sample, and investigation
   2 offers an all-positive preset as a legitimate constant fit.

8. **`intervalGeometry` returns `value` and `y` as separate fields.** Its first
   shape spread the input row and then overwrote `y` with a screen coordinate, so
   a figure reading `row.y` for the observed response got a pixel. Found by the
   geometry assertions.

9. **Only the assessment row identifiers reach the browser.** The generated data
   module names the other three roles by size and does not list their rows.

10. **Figure 10 is a table, not a drawing.** The specification allows a schematic
    "without invented realizations, or a specifically recorded continuous-score
    simulation before showing empirical trajectories". No such simulation was
    run, so no trajectories are drawn; the three quantities are separated in a
    table with the Beta mean and the binomial layer stated beside it with their
    conditions.

11. **The falsification harness lives in `scripts/`, not `scratch/`.** A harness
    in a gitignored directory cannot be re-run from a clean checkout.

### The exact rank is load-bearing, and shown to be

`Math.ceil((n + 1) * (1 - alpha))` in ordinary floating point returns the wrong
integer at **26 points of the grid these controls admit**, including n=24 at
alpha=.44, where it asks for rank 15 of 14 available scores. The rank is
therefore computed in integers from alpha's decimal representation, and the
models verifier recomputes all 98,200 (n, alpha) pairs by scanning integers
under exact rational arithmetic. The 26 disagreements are recorded in the
evidence file so the guard is shown to bite inside the reachable grid rather
than in principle.

### Found by looking

1. **The count-rail bars were drawn over the x-axis tick labels.** The bars grew
   upward from twenty units under the axis; the labels sit fourteen under it.
   Every number was right and the labels were covered. Nothing offline could see
   it: the shared layout inspector reads `<line>` elements and the curve sampler
   reads paths, and neither looks at a `<rect>` against a `<text>`.

2. **A one-row bin was indistinguishable from an empty one.** The bar length is
   the quantity and carries no minimum, which is right — and so a bin holding
   one row of fifty drew half a unit, exactly like the dashed marker for a bin
   holding none. The rail's whole job on this page is that "a dot without its
   denominator hides that difference". The count is now printed under each bar:
   the measured sigmoid bins are 50, 0, 1, 1, 28, and two of its five dots stand
   on a single observation each, which is now legible instead of being hidden by
   the very encoding meant to reveal it.

3. **Gold text on the gold winning bar.** In the temperature figure the label
   inside the leading segment used the shared light grey and was unreadable
   against the fill. Right size, inside its own bar, correct content — no
   geometry check can see a contrast failure.

4. **A stale screenshot from an earlier figure count.** Converting one figure
   from SVG to HTML reduced the figure count by one, and
   `calibration-figure-17-desktop.png` survived showing a layout that no longer
   existed — a picture of an already-fixed defect sitting in the evidence
   directory looking current. Distinct digests among the files a run *writes*
   say nothing about a file it no longer writes.

5. **A figure with no capture at all.** Once the label-roles figure became HTML
   it had no `.cal-figure`, so nothing in the evidence showed it. Three figures
   were in that position. Every complete figure block is now captured as well.

6. **The verdict compared two different quantities and named them both by the
   second.** Switching the graded summary to AUC and editing a card in the same

### Observations

**O1 — the rotation verdict's rank equivalence is false under ties.** Fixed. The
sentence is now conditional on the scores being distinct; when ties are present
the verdict says instead that the rank test holds in one direction only, because
a card whose rank exceeds the target can still be covered under the weak
comparison. **O2 — investigation 4's null explanation attributes the null to the
wrong cause in two of three branches.** Fixed. A `scalesOnlyChanged` helper
distinguishes "only the local scales moved" from "nothing that matters moved",
and the explanation is now branch-aware: in the absolute and CQR branches it
says the local scales are not used by that score at all, rather than appealing
to an order statistic that never saw a changed value. **O3 — one percentage
without its denominator.** Fixed: "76 of 80", matching the parallel sigmoid
sentence three lines earlier.

**O4 — a three-pixel clip at 320 px.** Accepted as a finding, not fixed. Two
visible KaTeX spans reach x = 323 in a 320 px viewport; the page does not scroll
sideways, so the overflow is clipped rather than lost, and the affected glyphs
are the outer edge of a delimiter rather than a digit or an operator. Narrowing
the formula further would cost a line break inside a bound. Recorded rather than
chased. **O5 — a one-row count bar is a hairline.** Accepted, not fixed. The

### Recorded disagreement with the packet (not edited)

The manuscript's `a≈.2677017731` for the frozen eight-score Platt fixture is
**wrong in its tenth decimal**. The true minimiser, verified independently at 60
digits, is

    a* = 0.267701773035583449394865993711…      b* = −a*/2

The manuscript's value is off by **5.2×10⁻¹¹**; the implementation's damped

### The stylesheet reaching into KaTeX's own markup

Carried in from a sibling lesson's review rather than from this one. A lesson
stylesheet that writes `.cal-lesson svg { height: auto }` also matches the
`<svg>` elements **KaTeX itself emits** for accents, radicals and stretchy
delimiters, which KaTeX sizes with `.katex svg { height: inherit }`. Equal
specificity, lesson sheet later, lesson sheet wins. In the sibling that
collapsed every radical to zero height and printed a bound as its own radicand,
with correct DOM, no `.katex-error` and every offline verifier green.
`calibration-labs.css` carried the same shape.

Measured on the built page at 1366, 390 and 320 px before changing anything.
This lesson renders 169 `.katex` nodes, exactly one of which emits an SVG: the
`\widehat` over `\mathrm{ECE}`. It is **not collapsed** — 9.078 px at all three
widths. But it is not right either. Cloning the same rendered formula outside
the lesson root, where KaTeX's stylesheet still applies and the lesson's does
not, gives **4.641 px**. The rule was drawing the accent at a factor of
**1.956**: the hat over the *empirical* ECE painted at twice its intended size,
on a page whose argument is that the empirical and the population quantity are
different objects.

Fixed by scoping every rule to the SVG's own class, `svg.cal-plot`. This is not
the ancestor-narrowing that the stylesheet's rule 1 forbids — the ancestor is
still the lesson root, so a diagram inside an investigation wrapper is still
reached; only the element is now named, and all six SVGs the lesson draws
already carried the class. Two guards were added, and the second is what makes
the first durable:

- **Hygiene.** The existing check "no blanket paint rule that would beat a
  presentation attribute" became "no stylesheet rule that reaches into markup
  this lesson does not own", with the bare-`svg` ban as its second member —
  the same family, so it belongs inside that check rather than beside it. It
  also now requires every `<svg>` in the components to carry `cal-plot`, since
  the scoping is worthless without that.
- **Browser.** Every `.katex svg` is measured at every layout state and must be
  within 0.5 px of the box KaTeX gave it. The assertion is the invariant, not
  today's number, so it catches a collapse to zero and an inflation alike.

Both falsified by reinstating the unscoped rule: the hygiene checker names the
selector and the line, and the browser verifier reports the ratio.

### Guard-domain audit of all six verifiers

The review could not complete this. Every guard in all six was read and asked
one question: *what shape of defect would this miss?* Where the answer was "a
real one", the guard was widened and then falsified. Four guards were found
that could not fail at all.

**`verify-calibration-sources.py`.** Four `check(True, …)` calls stood where
assertions should have been, two of them inside a loop over 19 files — so **43
of the 78 headline "checks" were assertions with no condition**. Each is now a
floor on what the scan actually looked at. Worse, the escape scan could not
catch the defect it was written for: it allows any alphanumeric after a
backslash, and an eaten backslash turns `'\\alpha'` into `'\alpha'`, where `\a`
is alphanumeric. A new check requires every backslash inside a math literal to
belong to an even-length run. Its own first draft matched whole
`<Math>{'…'}</Math>` elements and so saw 136 of 169 — the other 33 build their
content by concatenation — and matching the element region then every literal
inside it closed that. Two further defects surfaced while working there: the
rule scan was reading the stylesheet's own header comment as rules, and every
reported line number was 1 because `^\s*` swallowed the preceding newlines.

**`verify-calibration-models.mjs`.** The held-out isolation guard was **inert**.
It filtered candidates with `fixtureNumbers.has(v) && Math.abs(v) > 2 &&
!Number.isInteger(v)`, and every number in the fixtures is an integer or carries
at most two decimals, so no fixture value could satisfy that filter: `leaked`
was empty by construction. Every probability is below 1 in magnitude, so the
entire held-out probability range sat outside the guard's domain — the page
could have printed all 200 test probabilities into the fixtures and this
assertion would still have passed. The subject set is now every held-out array
in the data module, found by walking it for a `test…` path segment (13 arrays,
923 values), and the coincidence filter is precision rather than magnitude: a
match at three or more decimals is a copied value whatever its size. Widening it
immediately produced a hit — the prior baseline emits the *training* base rate
103/240 on all 80 test rows, and `floatingBoundary` shares that same training
quantity on purpose — which is handled as a class (a constant array reveals
nothing about any individual row) with the exclusion pinned by name, not as an
exemption carved for one value.

**`verify-calibration-browser.cjs`.** Every layout assertion has the form
`deepEqual(findings, [])`, which is green in two different worlds: the page is
correct, or the guard found nothing to look at. A subject census now runs at
every one of the ten layout states, using the *same* queries the guards use so
it cannot drift away from them independently, with floors just under the
measured minima. It found a real one on its first run: the curve-through-label
sampler queried `path, polyline, polygon` and had **two shapes in the whole
lesson** to sample, because almost everything this lesson draws is a `<line>`.
The docstring named the right hazard and the selector covered the other half of

contrast guard was measuring **7 of 175 labels** — only those over a filled
shape — so it was judging under five per cent of the text on the page; it now
falls back to the painted page background and takes 1,744 measurements per run.
The console-error assertion was green whether the console was clean or the
listener detached, so a deliberate `console.error` probe must now arrive and is
excluded by exact text from the judged set.

**`verify-calibration-examples.py`.** A dead `floats()` helper and its regex,
reachable from nothing, removed along with the import they alone needed.

**`verify-calibration-data.py`.** No inert guard found, and the reason is
structural rather than lucky: its comparison helper collects leaf coverage as it
compares, and coverage is asserted at 5,376 of 5,376 leaf paths with none
uncovered, which excludes the vacuity class by construction. Its one
`except` records a failure rather than swallowing it.

**`scripts/falsify-calibration.mjs`.** Four breakages pointed at CSS selectors

**One class common to four of the five.** A counter reported in the output and

### What the widened curve guard found on the page

Adding `<line>` to the curve sampler took its subject set from 2 shapes to 228
and produced eight findings, every one of them real.

1. **The gap drop through its own bin number.** In the opening reliability
   figure the bin number sits above its dot and the gap is drawn as a vertical

   through the digit. 30% of the line was inside the label. The number now goes
   to whichever side the drop is not on, with the viewBox-clipping rule winning

   a bin on the floor of the box with its drop going up has no free side at
   all. `labelBand` and `labelX` are computed in the model module so the rule is
   asserted over every reliability drawing rather than eyeballed on one.
2. **The decision threshold through four caption lines.** In the temperature

   which put a 1.6 px gold rule through the middle of each row's
   "entropy … nats · A clears the threshold". It is now one segment per bar,
   spanning the bar band only. The segments mark the same x on every bar, so the
   drawn rule is still exactly the applied rule.
3. **The rank label struck through by the threshold.** In the nine-score rail

4. **Two labels printed on the bars the rule crosses.** Allowed, explicitly and

   for those two classes. The allowance is granted only to the threshold rule
   and only for labels carrying `is-on-bar` or `is-on-fill`; every other shape
