**The five characteristic NMF caveats named in the brief were checked sentence by sentence and are all present:**

- **The factorization is not unique, and permutation and scale are not identified.** §4 carries the whole argument: the second exact factorization (`.jsx` L141–142), the explicit statement that `W₂/H₂` "are not simply the first components with their order or scale changed" and that "the observations alone have not identified which set of components is physically real", then permutation and scale as two *separate, simpler* ambiguities (L144) and the consequence that "a larger raw activation in one fit does not establish a stronger physical component than in another fit" (L145). `AmbiguityFigure` computes the containment from cone coordinates rather than drawing an impression, and its caption states "neither is a worse local minimum than the other." The blueprint's misconception list carries "The factorization is unique up to permutation and scale" as a *misconception*, which is the sharper framing.
- **Parts-based structure is encouraged by nonnegativity, not guaranteed.** The §1 `Callout` (L76–78) is a genuine single home: "Nonnegativity guarantees additive reconstruction. Recognizable physical parts, sparse factors, independence and a unique explanation require additional assumptions or evidence." It is not restated as a warning later; §5 uses it as mechanism ("That is stronger evidence than naming every component a digit part in advance", L165) and I3's own note repeats one line, which the specification itself requires.
- **The multiplicative update is not a descent proof for every loss.** §3 L133: "The original update's upper-bound argument establishes non-increasing objective values **under its mathematical conditions**. Objective decrease, stationarity, a local minimum and a global minimum are different statements." L134: "Adding an epsilon to every denominator or clipping factors after every step changes the algorithm … the unmodified proof cannot simply be copied onto that changed procedure." §7's majorization is explicitly derived for the Frobenius column problem with its positivity assumption stated ("Assume the fixed `W` has no all-zero component column and the current point `h'` is positive"). Nothing anywhere claims the guarantee extends to the β ≤ 1 updates. See O16 for the one thing neither the manuscript nor the page says.
- **The objective is nonconvex jointly and convex only in each factor alone.** §6 L174: "These are convex subproblems; alternating between them does not make the joint problem convex." §7 L198–199 gives the PSD Hessian for the fixed-factor problem and then the *computed* scalar counterexample, with the manuscript's own methodological guard preserved: "This directly tests the objective, rather than incorrectly concluding that any loss of a bilinear product must be nonconvex."
- **The reserved-image result is one comparison.** L163: "PCA wins this reconstruction comparison … This is a comparison of those representation choices, not equal storage bits or a digit-classification benchmark. There is no reason to tune the example until NMF wins." Repeated in `CandidateFigure`'s closing caption and in the closing paragraph L303. Nothing anywhere claims NMF won.

Also preserved and checked: labels "are never factorization features"; "Writer identities are unavailable in this extract, so this split concerns withheld images within the collection, not a claim about new writers"; "Repeatedly trying choices after viewing test results turns the test set into further validation"; "Neither an elbow nor a stable cluster assignment identifies a universal 'true number of topics'"; "A sparsity penalty is not a constraint that all component word lists become distinct"; "a hand-written λ is not automatically the same numerical parameter"; "Nonconvexity alone would not prove NP-hardness"; "A general unconstrained NMF fit is not automatically a separable model"; "normalizing arbitrary NMF factors after fitting does not supply that prior model or its posterior uncertainty"; "magnitude addition is a modeling approximation"; "Do not infer elapsed seconds from these operation counts"; and the closing "None of them is a benchmark or a claim about any future dataset."

### A4. Model and lab logic read for defects

- `updateH` / `updateW` (`nmf-models.js` L155–184) refuse a non-positive denominator with an explicit message rather than adding an epsilon, which is exactly what §3 and I2's note promise. Every supported preset keeps denominators positive — I confirmed the exact-fit case's `[14, 13, 27]` by hand.
- `sweep` (L187–196) applies `H` with the **old** `W` and then `W` with the **new** `H`, and returns `lossBefore`, `lossAfterH` and `loss` as three separate states. This is precisely what the F3 specification demanded and it is what the figure renders.
- `integerRank` (L307–331) is fraction-free Bareiss elimination on integers, so the rank-3 claim does not depend on a floating tolerance. It is correct — but no page component calls it (O9).

**S8. The §8 reconciliation sentence mis-describes what came out alphabetically, and the one genuinely surprising number in the output goes uncommented.**

Location: `src/learn/data/topics/non-negative-matrix-factorization-nmf.jsx` L226.

Evidence: what comes out alphabetically is the **vocabulary**, i.e. the four *columns*. The patterns are *rows*, and their order is not alphabetical — it is whatever the fit produced, which is the permutation ambiguity the next clause correctly points at ("notice which component the fit labels first"). As written, the sentence conflates a column ordering with a row ordering in the one paragraph whose job is to separate them. The manuscript's version had no such clause: "The exact vocabulary order is `['goal', 'orbit', 'rocket', 'team']`; each normalized nonzero pattern sums to 1."

Separately, I executed the program and confirmed the reconciliation the departure claims: component 1 is `[0.5, 0, 0, 0.5]` (goal + team) and component 2 is `[0, 0.5, 0.5, 0]` (orbit + rocket), so the fit's component 1 is the *sports* pattern while the manuscript's constructed component 1 is the *space* pattern. That is the most instructive thing in the whole output — an additive dictionary spreads a new document's mass onto its components' co-occurring words — and the page walks past it.

## Closure

The mathematics, the data handling and the numerical record of this lesson are, as far as I can establish, flawless. Every value I recomputed from first principles agrees to the full stated precision, and a large share of it agrees **exactly** rather than to a tolerance: the initial loss as `853/50`, the first H update as `110/53`, the whole `H⁽¹⁾` as rationals, the altered-X cells as `24/49` and `120/53`, the `H₁₁ = 3` contrast as `30/13`, the exact-fit null's unit ratios, the zero-lock gradient, practice 2's loss as `1/10`, practice 1's proportions as `1/4` and `3/4`, the ordinary rank of `S` by fraction-free elimination, and all six crossed-zero pairs by exhaustive enumeration. The digit pipeline reproduces from the raw CSV with a maximum absolute difference of **3.47e−18** against the shipped module and **0.0** against the packet's recorded native values: the seed-19 split membership, all eight fits with their iteration counts, the eight-row dictionary, the 60 reserved activation rows, the reserved comparison at `.069995 / .019678 / .025381` with **PCA ahead and nothing retuned**, source row 242 at `.021503642603`, all eight of its removals, component 8 at exactly `0.0`, and the reconstruction peak at `1.017674616` that makes the no-clipping rule necessary. All three displayed programs reproduce their published output byte-for-byte.
