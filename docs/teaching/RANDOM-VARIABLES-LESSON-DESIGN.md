## Learning sequence and conceptual contracts

1. **An outcome is not yet a number.** Use two labelled binary trials and list TT, TH, HT, HH with declared independent probabilities. Define a random variable as a fixed rule applied to an uncertain outcome, a realization as the obtained value and the law as probability collected by that rule. Different outcomes can map to the same value. Define events by preimages, not by assigning fresh equal weights to the displayed values. Introduce PMF, support and CDF, including zero-mass states and right-closed thresholds.
2. **Average the values with the correct weights.** Derive expectation on outcomes, regroup it by values and derive E[g(X)] directly. Explain finite integrability automatically and later infinite-space restrictions. Linearity does not require independence. Compare E[X²] with (E[X])² and use indicators to count without inventing an independence requirement. Expectation is a model average, not a promised next observation.
3. **A spread measure and a prediction criterion.** Center the variable, derive the two variance formulas and affine unit changes. Show E[(X−c)²] = Var(X)+(c−E[X])², so the mean is the best constant squared-loss prediction when the second moment is finite. Separate standard deviation, squared units, a tail event and a probability interval; equal mean/variance do not identify a law.
4. **Keep pairings before summarizing.** Build a joint table with marginals and conditional rows; positive marginal mass is required for finite conditioning. Give independence as the full joint-product equation, not a picture or one cell. The same marginals admit different joint laws. Then define covariance through signed centered products and derive the product-minus-means identity.
5. **What correlation does and does not say.** Derive covariance bilinearity, scale/sign behavior, correlation bounds using a nonnegative square, equality as an almost-sure affine relation under positive variances, and undefined correlation for constant variables. Explicitly exhibit dependent X and X² with zero covariance. A jointly Gaussian exception, if mentioned, must name joint normality, not merely normal marginals; its distribution theory is linked rather than assumed. Correlation does not establish an intervention effect.
6. **Combine measurements with the covariance term intact.** Expand Var(aX+bY), then the vector form E[(X−μ)(X−μ)ᵀ], positive semidefiniteness and AΣAᵀ with dimensions locally decoded. A matrix such as [[1,2],[2,1]] is impossible because the variance of X−Y would be negative. Use a complete original synthetic sensor model to show common and separate noise. Derive the best linear prediction coefficient as an optional direct application of the same quadratic, including Var(X)=0 and nonlinear conditional-mean limits.
7. **Information changes the prediction.** Derive finite conditional means as weighted cell averages and distinguish E[Y|G=g] from the random variable E[Y|G]. Derive tower, total variance and total covariance with the residual cross terms shown. Explain that averaged residual variance decreases with more allowed information, not necessarily every selected subgroup's variance. Use a within-group negative versus pooled positive covariance example; this is a distribution fact, not a causal model claim.
8. **Replace finite sums with a mass-preserving integral.** Reconnect PMF/CDF to density and units. Transform U uniform on [−1,1] through Y=U² using both inverse branches: F_Y(y)=sqrt(y), density 1/(2sqrt(y)) on (0,1), mean 1/3, variance 4/45. The density is unbounded near zero but has finite area; no probability atom is introduced by that graph shape. Include a short mixed-law reminder and link the fully taught Probability/Measure branches. General multidimensional Jacobians remain with their owners.
9. **Know when a requested moment exists.** Give a worked positive Pareto tail where the mean requires alpha>1 and second moment alpha>2, with the integral test visible. Contrast a symmetric principal value with an undefined signed expectation using Cauchy, without claiming a finite truncated computation proves integrability. Full limit and measure theory is linked.
10. **A sample is a random input to another statistic.** Distinguish population expectation/variance from a realized sample mean/covariance. Derive Var(mean) from all pair covariances; compare independent and fully copied readings with the same marginal. Explain iid finite-mean law-of-large-numbers conditions without promising any finite realization, and give a finite-variance Chebyshev derivation. Derive the n−1 correction for iid paired observations with finite second moments; it does not repair selection bias, arbitrary weights or dependence. Show a complete stable centered/Welford computation and an honest arithmetic contract.
11. **A full decision and independent transfer.** Finish a shared-noise combination with means, variances, covariance, units, finite outcomes and a conclusion about what cancels. Ask learners to change a noise assumption and recalculate. Local practice also contains a dice enumeration, a zero-covariance counterexample, a zero-probability conditional cell, a transformation with two preimages, a changed conditional decomposition, an invalid covariance matrix and a misuse of n−1. Each has a hint and an explained solution; only necessary extra proof depth is disclosed.

## Planned example fixtures and exact checks

### Outcome mapping

Two independent coins with head probabilities p and q have TT, TH, HT, HH masses (1−p)(1−q), (1−p)q, p(1−q), pq. The head count maps these to 0,1,1,2. At p=q=1/2 the PMF is 1/4,1/2,1/4. A first-coin indicator and an equal-outcome reward use the same outcome space with a different fixed function. Changing the mapping never resamples or loses mass. Endpoints p=0/1 remain valid degenerate laws.

### Center and squared loss

Use X in {−2,0,3} with weights 1/4,1/2,1/4: mean 1/4, second moment 13/4, variance 51/16. The mean is not an attainable value. A candidate c has expected squared loss 51/16+(c−1/4)². Show weighted contributions and changing residual lengths rather than only a scalar gauge.

### Joint-law comparisons

Use independent uniform, matching and opposite pairings on {−1,0,1}; the first three share both marginals. A separate clearly labelled nonlinear fixture takes X uniform on {−1,0,1} and Y=X²; covariance is zero, variance(Y)=2/9 and independence fails at an explicit cell. A zero scaling produces a constant Y and undefined correlation; do not display 0 as a correlation estimate. Finite shifts/scales demonstrate units and sign changes.

### Shared noise and combination

Let S be equally likely −2 or 2 and e_A,e_B independently equally likely −1 or 1, all three independent in this synthetic model. Define A=10+S+e_A and B=20+S+e_B in mV. There are eight equally likely outcomes. Means are 10 and 20; both variances are 5 mV², covariance 4 mV² and correlation 4/5. Var((A+B)/2)=9/2 mV², while Var(B−A)=2 mV². Averaging retains S; differencing removes it. Baselines describe two different fixed means, so their average is not automatically a better estimator of either mean. For a common unknown target, first state calibrated baseline removal and the actual estimand.

For linear combination aA+bB, separate noise lanes show (a+b)S, a e_A and b e_B. Optional minimum-variance weights for a common calibrated target may be included only with the sum-to-one constraint and singular/zero-noise cases handled. A full sensor-fusion algorithm or empirical electronics performance claim is not implied.

### Conditioning and a grouping reversal

Let G take −2 or 2, and U independently take −1 or 1. Set X=G+U, Y=G−U. At equal G mass, mean(X)=mean(Y)=0; Var(X)=Var(Y)=5; pooled covariance=3; each positive-mass G group has covariance −1. Total covariance is −1+4. E[Y|G]=G, so total variance is residual 1 plus between-group 4. Unequal group probability changes Var(G)=16p(1−p), exposing how mixing changes the pooled relation. At a zero-mass group, its conditional cell has no identified distribution and must be shown as undefined, not silently treated as an observed subgroup.

### Repeated-sample distribution

For Bernoulli(p), compare the exact distribution of the average of n independent draws with n copies of one draw. Both means are p; variances are p(1−p)/n and p(1−p). This is an exact theoretical comparison, not empirical simulation. The chart must explicitly say what is random across repetitions. A displayed sample estimate can be added in a separate labelled native calculation but must not replace the exact law.
