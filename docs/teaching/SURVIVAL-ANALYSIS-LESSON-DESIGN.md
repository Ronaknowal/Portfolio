The exact-topic inventory returned no individual blueprint and no incoming destination note. The unrelated resolved DSA inbox entry does not authorize other work. Current handoff, teaching standard, domain playbook, topic-design workflow, code standard, frontend-design skill/project design context and bounded verification/retention policy apply.

Proposed required prerequisites: **Linear & Logistic Regression** and **Hypothesis Testing & Confidence Intervals**. The current first prerequisite supplies features, fitting, losses, train/validation/test roles, probabilities and separation; its reviewed foundations supply vectors/probability. The second supplies test/interval meaning and assumptions, refreshed again beside Greenwood/log-rank. Locally teach likelihood contributions, conditional probability, products, derivatives as slopes and integration as area; link Maximum Likelihood & MAP and calculus for optional formal review. Do not silently require later evaluation, calibration, time-series, neural networks, causal inference or clinical training. Explain a survival tree split locally even though Decision Trees appears earlier. Deep networks remain an optional mechanism comparison, not a training prerequisite.

Core finish line: turn a clearly defined first-event process into valid observed records; explain censoring and risk sets; calculate and interpret KM, restricted means, log-rank and a Cox fit; choose a supported horizon; compare a frozen model to a baseline using held-out survival probabilities and ranking under stated censoring assumptions; identify situations that the model cannot answer. Intermediate work adds diagnostics, alternative hazard/time models and competing events. Deeper branches give derivatives, tie conventions, time-varying histories, conditional versus marginal hazards, interval/discrete likelihoods and high-dimensional/learned representations. This is a substantial end-to-end first-event lesson, not a claim to cover every recurrent-event or clinical causal method.

## Mechanism-first route

The central scenario is **one new pump, from commissioning to its first seal failure**, measured in days. A pump still running when logging ends has supplied an inequality about its lifetime. Eight named pumps support the hand calculations. A later, explicitly separate seeded 900-pump population supports fitted-model evaluation; its generator and independence assumptions are visible. The old 200-subject binary-feature experiment and Rossi example are retained as additional, clearly identified cases.

1. **Define the event and the clock.** One pump, time zero, units, event definition and feature availability. Distinguish eventual occurrence from event by a horizon. Draw actual event, censor and hidden continuation on separate lanes. Introduce T, C, Y=min(T,C), δ=I(T≤C). Specify event priority when recorded times tie; actual timestamp coarsening may warrant another observation model. Derive why δ is not a second independent classification target. Show the first complete data-encoding program with setup before its code.
2. **What an incomplete record tells us.** Independent subjects; censoring may be independent of T conditionally on measured X without being marginally independent. Explain overlap/positive follow-up at a requested horizon and administrative study-end versus difficulty-related withdrawal. Contrast treating censoring as failure and throwing away all censored rows. Give two different tail laws consistent with a common administrative cutoff to explain identification, not just estimator bias. Define left/interval censoring and truncation with literal intervals; defer their detailed likelihoods to the extension.
3. **Build a survival curve by counting.** For each distinct event time, count n just before the event and d failures; multiply previous survival by (n−d)/n. Include censored-at-same-time units in that risk set then remove them for later events. The risk-set cursor, lanes, table and staircase share one state. Explain the product through conditional probabilities; with no censoring it telescopes to empirical survival. Show full hand arithmetic at t=4 and the exact executable KM table. S(t)=P(T>t) throughout; use S(t−) before an atom. Do not silently swap left/right conventions copied from another source.
4. **Read uncertainty and the supported horizon.** Greenwood sum estimates Var(log S); multiply by S² for approximate Var(S), derive the log-log transform through a local derivative and show pointwise 95% limits. State the approximation, multiplicity and boundary cases. For all-censored data or an exhausted final risk set, a boundary library interval is not evidence of certainty; display an honest endpoint status rather than divide by zero. Define median not reached and area RMST(τ)=E[min(T,τ)] with a rectangles argument. Draw cutoff/area with censor markers and at-risk counts; a flat unsupported extension cannot identify the mean. Apply the same quantity to time-limited search, with success/time interpretation reversed appropriately.
5. **Hazard is a rate among survivors.** Start with a short interval and its changing denominator. Derive h=f/S and S=exp(−∫h) only for an absolutely continuous positive-lifetime law with S(0)=1. A rate may exceed 1 per time unit; interval probability is 1−exp(−integrated hazard), not h itself. Use Exponential and Weibull with explicit rate/scale/shape units, remaining-life probability S(a+Δ)/S(a), and no finite t=0 rate display for Weibull shape<1. Explain discrete hazards q_j and product survival separately; Nelson–Aalen sum is not exactly −log KM.
6. **Fit censored observations with the right likelihood.** For an event observe a density contribution f(Y), for a right-censored record a tail contribution S(Y), under an ignorable censor mechanism with separate nuisance parameters. Write δ log h(Y)−H(Y). Exponential MLE equals events/total observed exposure; explain how every censored running day still changes it. Distinguish event density from probability. No-events gives a boundary supremum at zero rate if the model requires positive rate. Add full Exponential and Weibull/AFT examples, explicit stable log terms, rather than relying on named APIs.
7. **Compare groups before regression.** Form one two-group event/risk table; under equal hazards, event labels have a hypergeometric conditional distribution. Sum O−E and variances; test statistic with asymptotic χ²1 reference. Give a complete changed table, original generator result and zero-information case. Explain why the test's null, a hazard ratio, a survival difference and causal effect are different questions. A declared earlier/later effect may cancel in log-rank; do not choose a new test after seeing the data without acknowledging selection.
8. **Cox learns relative hazard inside each risk set.** Introduce h(t|x)=h0(t)exp(xᵀβ), dimensionless score and conditional hazard ratio. At one untied event, normalize risk weights; baseline cancels from the infinitesimal one-event comparison. Multiply local contributions, not an unjustified ordinary classification likelihood over independent rows. Show no intercept identification, common-score-shift invariance and risk-set influence of censored units. Derive gradient as observed feature minus the risk-weighted mean, Hessian as negative weighted covariance; concavity alone does not ensure a finite unique maximizer. Rebuild the original NumPy fit as a standalone stable, safeguarded solver with objective/gradient/status output.
9. **Ties, baseline and absolute prediction.** Derive Breslow and Efron denominators from a visible tied failure set. State that Efron is an approximation for coarsened continuous events, distinct from the exact conditional subset calculation for a discrete model. Current library default and explicit `ties` arguments are checked. Recover Breslow H0, map centered library coordinates, and calculate S(t|x)=exp(−H0(t)exp(score)). HR=0.5 does not halve the failure probability. Actual model-based coefficient intervals are labeled conditional/asymptotic; Rossi is a historical descriptive example, not a recommendation about individuals.
10. **Check what proportional means.** Separate linear predictor shape, static PH, censoring, independence, and extrapolation. Use exact piecewise-hazard curves to show hazard-change time differs from survival-crossing time. Explain Schoenfeld residual x_event−risk-weighted mean and scaled trend diagnostics, then actually execute the library test/selected residual values on a declared dataset. A nonsignificant test does not prove PH. Stratification gives separate baselines and no coefficient for the stratum; time-varying coefficients differ from time-varying measurements. An optional mixture of two constant-rate types shows why conditional PH need not survive marginalizing over survivor composition.
11. **Evaluate the prediction you actually need.** Freeze subject/group/time split and train-only preprocessing. A training KM is a proper simple survival baseline. Risk ranking uses a comparable-pair matrix; high score means earlier failure. Explicitly include the scikit-survival event-versus-censor tie convention, exclude tied events, give half credit for tied scores and report no comparable pairs as undefined. C may be below .5; it does not establish calibration. For a horizon, derive Brier loss on I(T>τ), explain why censored-before-τ outcomes are unknown, and derive inverse observation weights with a tiny independent censoring law. Distinguish known G, fitted marginal G, conditional G(t|X), positivity and instability. Include Uno's truncated concordance as a qualified alternative, dynamic AUC as a horizon-specific pair question, and integrated Brier with declared grid/weighting. The core UI uses a finite support-separated censor example; it does not imply arbitrary event/censor atom conventions are interchangeable.
12. **Choose a different model deliberately.** AFT rescales time, so quantiles multiply; common-shape Weibull is both AFT and PH with opposite coefficient scaling, but covariate-dependent shape no longer gives one AFT multiplier. A survival tree chooses a log-rank split and predicts leaf KM/NA curves; bootstrap/random features and averaging build a forest. Averaging survival curves is not exponentiating averaged cumulative hazards. Show a tiny actual split/leaf diagram and complete RSF/AFT examples. Penalized Cox has a declared penalty, train-only feature selection and no automatic post-selection p-values. Optional discrete hazards connect to a complete person-period logistic program; DeepSurv learns g(x) but still PH, while discrete joint time/cause outputs need valid normalization, censor likelihood and tail treatment. No unverified empirical winner or algorithm-size cutoff.
13. **Competing events spend the same probability mass.** First seal failure versus permanent electrical failure: only one first-event type occurs. From one event-free state, distribute S(t−)d_k/n to each cause; remaining mass stays event-free. Show a exact finite CIF table, the wrong 1−KM comparison, and constant cause-specific hazard derivation. Cause-specific Cox removes other causes from later risk sets legitimately; derive actual CIF using all causes. Fine-Gray models f_k/(1−F_k), whose denominator includes those already failing from another cause; its coefficient is not a cause-specific HR or a simple risk ratio. Separate fitted Fine-Gray CIFs can be incoherent jointly; state the checked software boundaries. No assumption of independent latent failure times is needed to define the observed CIF, nor can removing a competing cause be inferred without a causal model.
14. **Respect entry, history and observation intervals.** A start/stop lane figure shows delayed entry L<t≤Y, a feature update and the inadmissible future measurement. Risk-set adjustment requires an appropriate truncation-independence/support assumption; it cannot invent a population's pre-entry history. Time-varying covariates are predictable histories, split by subject rather than row. Landmarking predicts within survivors at a chosen age and changes the estimand. Give exact count/probability programs and current CoxTimeVaryingFitter usage when useful. Optional interval likelihood F(R)−F(L), left F(R), and right S(L); show a small parametric interval fit, current `fit_interval_censoring`, and why endpoint conventions matter. Define repeated/recurrent-event scope and robust subject-level inference without pretending independent reset rows solve it.
15. **Produce a held-out reliability report.** Seeded machine data, explicit generating law, train600/validation150/test150, independent rows, predeclared test administrative cap12 days and evaluation grid2,4,6,8,10. Fit training KM plus a small declared Cox/feature/forest candidate list. Select using validation integrated Brier only, freeze, then report test Brier, ranking, support/at-risk counts and appropriate uncertainty or a clear single-split limit. A complete separate AFT fit provides current method transfer. Compare to known synthetic S only as an oracle, never as training/test information available in a real study. Discuss spare-part planning/conditional remaining-life and a stipulated fixed-horizon action threshold, separating forecasts from estimated intervention benefit.
16. **Practice and next steps.** Changed records, one KM multiplication/CI interpretation, an unsupported tail, conditional-rate units, censored MLE, log-rank null/variance, Cox weight/shift/tie cases, PH versus marginal mixture, pair/calibration/IPCW cases, AFT time scaling, competing mass, entry/future leakage and a changed held-out report. Every substantial task has an optional hint before a separate explained solution, including accepted changed-program results and reasons tempting shortcuts fail. Finish the supervised block honestly, then point to the route's actual next clustering topic and optional survival references.

Essential steps remain visible. Optional derivation, mixture, exact discrete tie, neural and interval-censor extensions are clearly signposted; they may be revisited after the first complete pipeline. The precise final heading grouping can change if the reading flow improves, but not by dropping promised mechanisms.
