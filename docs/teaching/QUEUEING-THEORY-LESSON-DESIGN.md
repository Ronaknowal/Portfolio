## First-time learner route and exact anchors

Use one service desk or job worker as the plain model, then explain why an API, a fabrication station and a repair backlog share the same counting mechanism. These are original teaching scenarios, not measured production results.

1. **Follow four jobs before naming a distribution.** Start empty. Arrivals `[0,1,2,6]` seconds and service requirements `[3,1,2,1]` give starts `[0,3,4,6]`, departures `[3,4,6,7]`, queue waits `[0,2,2,0]` and system times `[3,3,4,1]`. Tie policy: arrivals retain input order; departure frees the server before a simultaneous new service starts. Half-open residence intervals avoid double-counting at endpoints. Derive start=max(arrival, previous departure), then Lindley's workload recurrence. Explain service demand versus elapsed response before introducing mu.
2. **Count the same occupancy area two ways.** At horizon 7, area is 11 job-seconds, mean occupancy 11/7, admitted rate 4/7 and cohort mean system time 11/4. Waiting-only area is 4 job-seconds. At horizon 5, occupied area is 9 but completed-job residence sum is only 6; the unfinished job supplies the missing 3. Show why a naive completion-only product differs. Give the exact clipped-interval identity first and a properly qualified long-run statement second. Empty observations give no customer mean to divide by. Changing system boundaries must change all three quantities coherently.
3. **Explain a random arrival/service model.** Poisson counts and exponential gaps/services are assumptions, not consequences of knowing a mean. Clarify independence, stationarity and first-come-first-served (FCFS). A small birth/death graph connects a count n to arrival rate lambda and departure rate mu when n>0; the empty state cannot depart. PASTA means nonanticipating Poisson arrivals sample the time-state distribution; it does not make successive customers' delays independent or hold for arbitrary synchronized arrivals.
4. **Derive and use M/M/1.** Balance probability flow across n/n+1, obtain pi_n=(1-rho)rho^n and normalize only for rho<1. Derive the mean from the geometric series and then W/L/Wq/Lq, retaining the original 8/10 program. Explicitly compare offered rate, busy fraction and headroom. As lambda approaches mu from below, the stationary mean diverges; at or above the boundary the infinite-buffer stationary formulas are unavailable, not negative delays. A zero-rate system has no observed customer mean; any tagged-customer limiting interpretation must be named separately.
5. **Separate a mean from a tail.** Use random total duration T and waiting duration Tq; reserve W=E[T], Wq=E[Tq]. Under stationary FCFS M/M/1, condition on geometric arrival-seen occupancy and the remaining independent exponential stages. Derive P(T>t)=exp(-(mu-lambda)t) and P(Tq>t)=rho exp(-(mu-lambda)t) for t>=0, with mass 1-rho at Tq=0. A quantile can be zero for Tq; it is not obtained by blindly returning a negative logarithm. At lambda=.9,mu=1, queue p99 is about 44.9981 seconds and system p99 about 46.0517, distinct from mean 10. These are model quantiles, not guaranteed maxima or empirical estimates.
6. **See why an inspected job is not a typical job.** Keep E[S]=.1 seconds. Equal .05/.15 services retain the original variance example. A rare-long mixture with 95% .05-second and 5% 1.05-second jobs also has mean .1 but E[S²]=.0575. Long jobs are only 5% of jobs but 52.5% of occupied service time. Explain length bias and residual remaining time through rectangles/triangles rather than a formula-only variance slider.
7. **Derive P–K from residual work.** Under FCFS, independent iid service and independent Poisson arrivals, the arriving wait is unfinished current service plus whole queued service demands. The time-average residual (zero while idle) is lambda E[S²]/2; the residual conditional on being busy is E[S²]/(2E[S]). Do not conflate these. PASTA plus the independent unrevealed queued service requirements and Little's waiting-only law give Wq=R+lambda Wq E[S], then the stated formula. At lambda=8 the rare-long Wq is 1.15 seconds, while the original standard-deviation example gives .25. Develop constant/exponential comparisons and the rho<1/finite-second-moment assumptions alongside the result.
8. **Use pooling with an explicit capacity model.** Derive the M/M/c stationary weights with birth rate lambda and completion rate min(n,c)mu. Let a=lambda/mu and rho=a/c; define the probability of waiting C and obtain Wq=C/(c mu-lambda). At lambda=8,c=2,mu=5, pooled W=5/9 seconds; two independent equal-split M/M/1 queues give W=1 second. A single twice-as-fast worker has different per-job service time and gives .5 seconds. Thus equal total capacity does not imply equal latency, and the conclusion depends on exponential iid jobs/identical servers/routing. A small visual comparison should expose idle capacity stranded beside a busy separate queue.
9. **Distinguish bounded occupancy from successful service.** For M/M/1/K, K includes the in-service slot. Normalize pi_n proportional to rho^n over n=0..K, including the uniform rho=1 case. Offered arrivals can be rejected; lambda_eff=lambda(1-pi_K), busy fraction=1-pi_0, and admitted-customer mean W=L/lambda_eff. A finite chain can be stationary under offered rho>1 by dropping work; that does not satisfy an infinite-buffer latency promise. Retries can alter the offered rate, and batching/closed populations require another model.
10. **Check an operational decision.** Provide complete seeded FCFS simulation and event-area accounting, exact trace verification, burn-in/unfinished-cohort discussion, repeated independent runs and an explicitly finite stochastic comparison. Separate mathematical identities, model predictions and measured evidence. Retain the original 90/100 task, add changed unit/variability/quantile/finite-buffer and modeling tasks, and finish with the actual Dynamical Systems lesson. A short workload/drain feedback bridge belongs here; no detour through Random Matrix Theory.

Optional depth may include the stable-heavy-tail counterexample, M/G/1 busy-period mean versus wait, and repeated server vacations/slotting as a concrete residual-time application. The MIT vacation formula is only for its declared repeated iid independent vacation model; a sleeping GPU with load-dependent setup is not automatically that model. Choose the branches that add distinct understanding without obscuring the main route. General queueing networks, priority transforms, processor sharing, load-dependent service, heavy-traffic limit proofs and real LLM serving engineering remain follow-on scope.
