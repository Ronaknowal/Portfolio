## Quantitative contracts

Use γ=p/n consistently; many sources transpose the data matrix. With raw known-zero-mean data, the covariance estimate is p×p and has rank at most min(n,p). Sample centering reduces the available row rank to n−1. For Gaussian observations, an orthogonal basis of the centered row subspace gives an exact Wishart law with n−1 degrees of freedom; for non-Gaussian data, do not silently claim the transformed rows are independent. Empirical standardization is another operation, not an automatic iid assumption.

The MP measure has edges σ²(1±√γ)² for all γ>0; its positive continuous part has mass min(1,1/γ), with the remaining mass at zero. At γ=1 the density has an integrable singularity at zero. The plotted bin values should integrate the density, or explicitly omit an endpoint while explaining the singularity; no fake finite endpoint height, deleted zero mass or conditional-positive histogram presented as an unconditional law. “The smallest eigenvalue” and “the smallest nonzero eigenvalue” must remain different when p>n.

State bounded-support/finite-moment assumptions at their respective claims. The iid finite-variance bulk theorem does not by itself establish convergence of the largest eigenvalue; stronger extreme-value conditions are separate. Finite simulations illustrate a theorem and numerical behavior but do not prove the theorem or establish a real-data false-positive rate.

For the Gaussian bound, keep n≥p, δ∈(0,1), probability at least1−δ and max(0,√n−√p−t) explicit. It is a bound, not an equality or optimal confidence interval. Numerical integration near a singular endpoint, eigenvalue tolerances and accepted dimensions/seeds must have explicit contracts. Reject malformed, non-finite and unsupported arithmetic states rather than silently returning zero, NaN or an apparently precise direction.

The spike theorem uses ℓ as the population eigenvalue and ℓ−1 as the additive strength; never mix those thresholds. Keep Gaussian, fixed one-spike, simple outlier and asymptotic aspect-ratio assumptions beside the formulas. Finite alignment is a random observation. Rotations/signs of numerical eigenvectors require sign-invariant overlap or projectors. Statistical impossibility for arbitrary estimators/structured priors is outside this theorem.

For the upper spike, the limiting squared overlap is0 when ℓ≤1+√γ and otherwise [1−γ/(ℓ−1)²]/[1+γ/(ℓ−1)]. The sample leading value tends to b below the threshold and to ℓ(1+γ/(ℓ−1)) above it. The random-linear-map example uses a d×d W with independent mean-zero entries of variance1/d and a fixed deterministic x; expanding the squared norm establishes its expectation. Do not silently replace “fixed before the draw” by a maximizing direction chosen after observing W.

The two-by-two gap formula is a finite GOE result. After mean-gap rescaling it gives the usual two-by-two surmise; do not label that formula the exact spacing law for every large GOE. Spectral bulk, edge and local gap questions should be explicitly distinguished.
