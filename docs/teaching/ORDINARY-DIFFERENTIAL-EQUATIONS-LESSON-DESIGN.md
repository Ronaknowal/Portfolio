## Learning flow, concrete examples and proof obligations

The headings below are a mechanism plan, not a fixed template or mandatory number of sections/labs. Preserve complete core arguments if the eventual reading flow combines stages. Longer proofs and specialist branches can disclose progressively; conditions necessary to interpret a formula stay next to it.

### 1. Specify what changes and what is known

Start with a synthetic heated object in a fixed-temperature room. Its current excess temperature is x, not its heat-loss rate. A lumped thermal balance is C dx/dt=P(t)−Gx: capacity C has J/K units, heat input P has W, and conductance G has W/K. For C=600 J/K, G=2 W/K and time measured in minutes, the equation becomes x'=.1P(t)−.2x, with x in K and x' in K/min. Set P=0, x(0)=40 and ask when the excess is halved. Explicitly label the fixed-room, constant-parameter, lumped-state assumptions; this is a teaching model, not measured device validation.

Use a balance picture where each labelled energy flow maps to one signed term, followed by a small tangent calculation: at x=40, the cooling rate is −8 K/min, but the exact next-minute loss is not 8 K. Define ordinary (one independent variable), order, scalar/system, autonomous/time-dependent, linear/nonlinear and initial condition only as each distinction becomes useful. “Linear” concerns the unknown state and derivatives, not whether the solution is a straight line. A time-varying coefficient can still give a linear equation. The initial state selects a trajectory; a rate law alone is not a complete prediction.

Define a classical solution on an interval: the state remains inside the stated domain, is differentiable there, satisfies the equation and the initial value. Checking a candidate means checking all four. A rate becomes accumulated change through x(t)=x0+integral f(s,x(s))ds; the unknown appears inside that integral. Contrast solving the equation with merely substituting a starting number into f.

### 2. Read the rate field before finding a formula

Show a t-versus-x direction field. Each short segment represents the slope f(t,x) of a possible solution at that location; it does not depict a one-minute state jump. Overlay the exact cooling curves for several initial values and a selected tangent, with numeric derivative and coordinate readouts. A frozen default picture must communicate this without interaction.

For autonomous scalar equations build a phase line by marking f(x)=0 and signs between zeros. Logistic growth, x'=r x(1−x/K), gives a useful contrast with unbounded exponential growth. Define K>0, r>0 and nonnegative modeled states. x=0 and x=K are equilibria; 0<x<K rises, x>K falls. A solution cannot cross an equilibrium under the local uniqueness assumptions. For time-dependent equations, curves crossing in the state-only projection or at different times do not contradict uniqueness.

### 3. Ask whether the rule determines a solution

State the usable local theorem precisely: on an open neighborhood of (t0,x0), f is continuous and locally Lipschitz in state, uniformly over a sufficiently small time neighborhood. Then the IVP has a unique local solution. Define the Lipschitz inequality as a bound on how much rates differ when states differ; a continuous state Jacobian is an easy sufficient condition. It is sufficient, not necessary. Continuity alone gives local existence in finite dimensions, but not uniqueness.

Show distinct failure mechanisms:

- x'=x^2, x(0)=1 is smooth yet x=1/(1−t) exists only up to t=1 forward. Its maximal interval through zero is (−infinity,1), not the disconnected union obtained by plotting the formula on both sides of the pole.
- x'=2 sqrt(abs(x)), x(0)=0 allows waiting: x(t)=0 for t<=a, then (t−a)^2 for t>a, for every a>=0. Check the derivative at the join, not just on each open piece. An Euler run stuck at zero selects one solution; it does not prove uniqueness.
- t x'=1 cannot hold at t=0, regardless of an initial value. Division by t does not repair the domain.

Explain continuous dependence separately from long-term stability. On a common interval with Lipschitz constant L, two solutions satisfy the Gronwall estimate norm(delta x(t))<=exp(L(t−t0)) norm(delta x0). This is an upper bound, often loose; it is not proof of actual exponential growth or decay. Present an optional proof from the integral equation and scalar integrating-factor comparison. A local existence theorem does not promise a global trajectory, accurate numerical computation or correct physical modeling.

Optional construction: Picard iteration refines an entire candidate function, not a single time step. Starting with x0(t)=1 for x'=x yields 1, 1+t, 1+t+t^2/2, ... . Explain the local rectangle/self-map and contraction or factorial-bound mechanism before claiming convergence; the explicit exponential example alone is not the general proof. Keep this distinct from numerical marching.

### 4. Construct scalar solutions without losing exceptional cases

Derive separation for x'=a(t)b(x) on a region where b is nonzero by differentiating an antiderivative of 1/b(x). Identify b(x)=0 equilibrium solutions first; do not lose x=0 by dividing by x. Work cooling, logistic growth and the blow-up example, always apply the initial condition and state the interval/physical domain. For logistic growth use a form that handles x0=0 directly, and explain x0=K; a cancellation-prone formula or an implicit logarithm should not silently discard these values.

Develop a nonseparable linear equation y'+p(t)y=q(t). Construct mu(t)=exp(integral from t0 to t of p), so (mu y)'=mu q. Integrating with initial data yields a definite formula without ambiguous constants. Work y'+2y/(1+t)=1+t, y(0)=1 on t>−1: y=(1+t)^2/4+3/[4(1+t)^2]. Verify both sides and the initial condition. It exists throughout that coefficient interval, but not across the singular endpoint.

Return to the object with constant P=20 W: x'=2−.2x, so x(t)=10+(x0−10)exp(−.2t). Interpret equilibrium, transient and time constant; from x0=40 the five-minute value is 10+30/e K. Differences of two solutions of the same forced linear problem satisfy the homogeneous equation. Their sum generally doubles the forcing. Define homogeneous solution plus one particular solution as an affine family, not an unrestricted vector-space superposition of forced solutions.

Optional method-selection branch: derive z=y^(1−n) for the Bernoulli equation y'+p y=q y^n with n!=0,1, on an explicitly nonzero/real-power domain, and check omitted zero solutions separately where the original power is defined. The n=0/1 equations are already linear. Contrast with an exact differential M(t,y)dt+N(t,y)dy=0: a potential F gives dF/dt=M+Ny'=0, so the solution follows a level set. Define the needed partial derivatives locally and check equality of cross partials for C1 coefficients on a suitable simply connected region; it is not an arbitrary global guarantee on a punctured domain. Use the small potential F=t^2+ty+y^2, then the branch issue near N=0. These are purposeful recognition tools, not a list of unexplained recipes.

### 5. Store enough state for second-order motion

Build m q''+c q'+k q=F(t), with position q, velocity v=q', mass m>0, damping c>=0 and stiffness k>0. Draw forces and their directions before the equation; distinguish kg, N, N·s/m and N/m. Then derive q'=v and v'=(F−cv−kq)/m. The initial condition needs both q0 and v0; identical positions with opposite velocities select different trajectories. Explain the general nth-order-to-first-order construction when the highest derivative is explicitly determined, and do not apply it blindly to a singular mass matrix or implicit constraint.

Derive the characteristic equation by substituting e^(lambda t). For real roots, repeated roots and conjugate roots, show enough differentiation to justify the real solution forms. A repeated root produces (a+bt)e^(lambda t); derive it by reduction of order, not by memorized exception. For a conjugate pair alpha±i beta, use a real sine/cosine basis and a local explanation of rotation; complex exponentials are an optional shorthand, not a hidden prerequisite.

For m=1, k=4, q0=1, v0=0, compare c=0,2,4,6. Define underdamped, critically damped and overdamped through the discriminant and actual motion. Critical damping is not an unconditional fastest response for every input, starting velocity or chosen performance metric. Link displacement/time, velocity/time and q-v state space with the same selected instant. Energy E=(m v^2+k q^2)/2 obeys E'=Fv−cv^2; derive it. A zero instantaneous energy derivative at a turning point does not mean undamped motion or a constant trajectory.

### 6. Build the solution operator for a coupled system

Use a two-compartment transfer model: x1'=-a x1+b x2, x2'=a x1−b x2, with a,b>=0, amounts in the same units, and x as a column. Each outgoing flow appears with opposite signs in the two balance equations; total amount is conserved. For a=1,b=2 and total 9, equilibrium is (6,3); from (9,0), the deviation is 3 exp(−3t)(1,−1). Explain conservation before interpreting a zero eigenvalue. A continuum of equilibria indexed by total is different from one globally attracting origin.

Derive x'=Ax from its rows, then use independent eigenvectors to decouple when possible: x=Vz gives z'=Lambda z. The initial coordinates solve V z0=x0; transpose only works for an orthonormal basis. Singular A does not prevent unique IVP solutions. Introduce the matrix exponential through its power series, bound it with a submultiplicative norm by the scalar exponential, and justify termwise differentiation on bounded time intervals. It satisfies E(0)=I, E'=AE and E(s+t)=E(s)E(t), so x(t)=exp(A(t−t0))x0. Exponentiating entries is a different operation.

Explain a fundamental matrix as columns obtained by evolving independent initial basis states. For continuous A(t), linear uniqueness keeps these columns independent throughout the coefficient interval. Normalization gives Phi(t,t0)=X(t)X(t0)^(-1). Keep multiplication order and the distinction between a matrix of solutions and one solution vector explicit. No inverse of A is required.

### 7. Read modes, defective coupling and stability boundaries

Use a linked plane/basis/evolution picture rather than only eigenvalue badges. Offer bounded exact real families: a node, a saddle, a rotation/spiral, a repeated diagonal matrix and a Jordan shear. In a Jordan example A=[[-1,3],[0,-1]], x0=(0,1), solve x2=e^(−t), x1=3t e^(−t). The factor t is accumulated forcing from the other component, not an extra eigenvalue. For A=[[0,1],[0,0]], exp(tA)=I+tA is unbounded for some starts although both eigenvalues are zero.

State fixed finite-dimensional constant-matrix results separately: all eigenvalues with strictly negative real parts are equivalent to exponential stability; any positive real part gives instability; boundedness when all real parts are nonpositive also requires semisimple imaginary-axis eigenvalues. Explain semisimple in terms of the absent growing Jordan coupling and illustrate zero and rotation. A trajectory initialized entirely in a stable invariant subspace need not reveal an unstable mode. Negative eigenvalues do not imply monotone decay in an arbitrary norm; preserve the early Dynamical36 transfer insight with a new worked shear and meaningful coordinate scaling.

For nonlinear f near an equilibrium, an appropriate differentiable Jacobian supplies a local test only. Compare x'=-x^3 and x'=x^3 at zero: identical zero derivative, different stability. Link Dynamical36 for deeper nonlinear behavior; do not repeat its chaos/bifurcation machinery.

### 8. Add inputs by propagating their contributions

Derive variation of constants from x(t)=exp(A(t−t0))c(t), obtaining x(t)=exp(A(t−t0))x0+integral exp(A(t−s))b(s)ds. Explain the integration variable as the time at which an input enters and the kernel as how long it then evolves. The full state is the initial response plus all propagated inputs. A thermal forcing timeline can show the contribution from one selected interval and its exact contribution at the current time; the total must reconcile with the complete state.

Teach piecewise constant input using a continuous state carried across each switch; the derivative can jump. Compute the interval update with Ad=exp(hA) and Bd=integral_0^h exp(A tau)B d tau. The inverse form A^(-1)(Ad−I)B requires A nonsingular and may be a poor numerical evaluation. Derive the augmented block exponential and include A=0 as a meaningful valid case: x_next=x+hBu. Distinguish specified external input, initial state and measured output y=Cx+Du. Direct feedthrough is counted exactly once. Keep S4/Mamba model claims in the destination note.

A focused sinusoidal-force branch derives the real particular coefficients for m q''+c q'+k q=F0 cos(omega t). With c>0 the steady-state amplitude is F0/sqrt((k−m omega^2)^2+(c omega)^2), with phase determined by both signed coefficients. It is the asymptotic periodic response after transients, not the full trajectory for arbitrary initial data. For c=0 at omega=sqrt(k/m), show the resonant t sin(omega t) term and why division by k−m omega^2 fails. Damped peak frequency need not equal the undamped natural frequency; if a frequency sweep is shown, calculate the actual peak condition, not a decorative symmetric curve. Avoid the incorrect Tacoma Narrows pure-resonance story.

### 9. Preserve time ordering when coefficients change

For continuous A(t), derive the general formula x(t)=X(t)[X(t0)^(-1)x0+integral X(s)^(-1)b(s)ds]. It uses the homogeneous fundamental matrix; it is not a closed elementary algorithm for arbitrary A(t). Explain why exp(integral A(t)dt) is not generally valid when matrices do not commute.

Use two exact unit-duration stages U=[[0,1],[0,0]] and V=[[0,0],[1,0]]. From (1,0), U then V gives (1,1); V then U gives (2,1), despite the same integral U+V. The stage product's later action is on the left. This piecewise-constant example is a stated extension through coefficient jumps, and the state is continuous at the switch. Provide a continuous example or a clearly stated sufficient pairwise-commutation condition when explaining the smooth theorem. This is an illuminating connection to ordered transformations, not an excuse to silently replace a time-varying system with averaged coefficients.

Optional ordinary-point series construction: in y''=t y with y(0)=1,y'(0)=0, substitute y=sum a_n t^n and align powers. Obtain a2=0 and (n+2)(n+1)a_(n+2)=a_(n−1) for n>=1, giving 1+t^3/6+t^6/180+... . Explain how the initial data fix the two free coefficients, why analytic coefficient hypotheses matter, and check a changed second solution. A truncated series has a truncation domain/error question; finite residual agreement is not a proof of convergence at every input. Further Frobenius and singular points remain explicitly signposted, with a saved scope note.

### 10. Approximate an IVP and keep the error contract visible

Derive forward Euler from the integral equation and a left-endpoint rate. Under a Lipschitz constant L and bounded second solution derivative M on the relevant region, show the one-step exact-start defect <=M h^2/2 and the accumulated recurrence e_(n+1)<=(1+hL)e_n+M h^2/2. With exact initial data over T=n h, derive the O(h) bound M h [exp(LT)−1]/(2L), using M T h/2 at L=0. State differentiability, common interval, arithmetic exclusion and the distinction between this sufficient bound and observed error. “Local truncation error” conventions can divide by h; declare the convention used here.

Derive explicit midpoint from an estimated half-step state, showing why a second stage samples a different rate. Give the full classical RK4 stage rule with names, times, states and weighted increment; motivate cancellation of lower-order error terms and verify representative low-degree conditions. State fourth-order global accuracy only for sufficiently smooth problems on a fixed interval under the usual stability assumptions. Do not imply four arbitrary slopes automatically make fourth order. An embedded/adaptive solver estimates local error to choose steps; it does not turn rtol into a global trajectory certificate.

The exact cooling multiplier exp(−kh), Euler 1−kh and backward Euler 1/(1+kh) give separate decay, positivity and accuracy questions. Euler decays strictly only for 0<kh<2; remains nonnegative for 0<=kh<=1 with nonnegative initial state; kh=2 alternates without decay. Backward Euler is stable for this negative real test mode for every h>0, but a huge step remains inaccurate. For general linear systems use the method's stability function on h lambda with the same defective-boundary caution. A stable step does not imply a good oscillation phase or conserved energy.

### 11. Recognize stiffness and handle real solver outcomes

Use the manufactured scalar problem y'=-lambda(y−cos t)−sin t, y(0)=1, whose exact solution is cos t. Perturbations decay with rate lambda even though the displayed solution varies slowly; explicit stability can force tiny steps. Also use a genuinely excited fast transient by changing y0. Define stiffness operationally in relation to method, interval and accuracy goal, not merely a large derivative or eigenvalue ratio. Compare actual RK45 and Radau runs at declared tolerances with analytic errors and evaluation counts; results are local computations, not universal speed rankings or fabricated timings.

Teach the actual solve_ivp contract: state shape, `(t,y)` callable, integration horizon, returned status/message, output sampling versus internal steps, relative/component absolute tolerances, Jacobian option where useful, and event direction/termination. A success flag includes termination by event. t_eval controls stored output, not all internal time steps. A sign-change detector can miss touching roots or multiple crossings inside a step. Demonstrate a threshold event with an analytic thermal hitting time and a deliberate missed-event fixture; a smaller max_step addresses this chosen crossing example, not every possible event.

At a known input discontinuity, split the solve at the switch and carry the state; do not demand a smooth solver step across an unannounced forcing jump. Clip or stop at an explicit physical/domain limit rather than draw through blow-up. Model inputs, floating-point arithmetic and computed reference uncertainty need separate labels. Reject invalid shape, nonfinite values, unusable step budgets and step sizes that cannot advance the floating-point clock. Never silently present a truncated run as horizon completion.

### 12. Distinguish initial data, boundary data and applications

An IVP supplies all initial state coordinates at one time. A boundary-value problem can specify values at different times and may have zero, one or many solutions despite perfectly smooth coefficients. For y''+y=0 with y(0)=0, y(pi)=0 allows every y=b sin t, whereas y(pi)=1 has no solution. On a nonresonant interval such as pi/2, y(pi/2)=1 gives a unique b=1. State exact pi versus a rounded numerical endpoint; a tiny residual at an approximate endpoint does not change the exact theorem. Full boundary spectra and Sturm–Liouville theory belong to the later boundary/eigenfunction branch.

Use two or three developed transfers rather than a list of industries. The thermal object connects rate/forcing/event time. The two-compartment system shows a conservation mode and can represent amounts or, after explicit normalization/transposition, a finite probability law. A spring response shows position/velocity state and forcing energy. The nilpotent held-input system is a less obvious link to sampled integrators and learned sequence-state updates. State each model's assumptions and avoid medical dose recommendations or unverified physical-performance claims. A changed thermal or compartment capstone must combine construction, analytical prediction, solver validation and interpretation.
