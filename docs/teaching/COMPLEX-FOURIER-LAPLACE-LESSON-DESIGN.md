Begin with a synthetic sensor voltage containing two oscillations. Seeing a complicated time trace does not reveal how much of each oscillation is present or how a slow sensor changes it. Default throughout the harmonic/filter examples:

Explain any later fixture change, especially the four-sample hand DFT and off-bin window example.

At completion, a learner should be able to:

- Compute and geometrically interpret complex addition, multiplication, division, conjugation, powers and roots, preserving quadrant and branch information.
- Convert a sinusoid into a complex phasor and explain amplitude, phase, negative frequency and conjugate symmetry.
- Derive frequency coefficients by cancellation/orthogonality, reconstruct a periodic signal and distinguish a finite approximation from convergence at or near a jump.
- Choose among Fourier series, the continuous Fourier transform, DTFT and DFT, with correct units, exponent sign and normalization.
- Compute a small DFT/inverse, explain one radix-two decomposition, and verify reconstruction and energy identities independently of an FFT library.
- Diagnose aliasing, leakage and zero padding as different effects; retain phase and distinguish bin spacing from observation duration and estimation uncertainty.
- Derive convolution as shifted responses, distinguish linear from circular convolution, and interpret a filter's gain, phase and initial transient.
- Construct and invert a unilateral Laplace transform for a piecewise-forced initial-value problem, and interpret its poles under explicit convergence/causality assumptions.
- Explain why the same rational expression can have different bilateral inverse signals, why an imaginary-axis substitution may be invalid, and why a transfer function alone can hide internal modes.
- Solve a changed acquisition/filter problem and diagnose what the result cannot establish.

## Mechanism-first reading progression

### 1. A second coordinate makes rotations computable

Start with a quarter-turn of a real-axis arrow: applying the same quarter-turn twice negates it. Introduce `i² = −1` as consistent arithmetic on pairs, not as a mysterious physical quantity. Derive `(a+ib)(c+id)=(ac−bd)+i(ad+bc)`, connect it to the real matrix `[[a,−b],[b,a]]`, and distinguish addition's translated arrows from multiplication's scaling/rotation. Use `(1+2i)(2−i)=4+3i`; division by `2−i` returns `1+2i`. Derive conjugate multiplication and division's nonzero-denominator condition. Modulus is a nonnegative length, zero only at the origin; `Im(z)` is the real coefficient of i. Complex numbers are not an ordered field extending the real ordering.

Introduce polar form from coordinates: `r=hypot(a,b)`, an angle determined modulo `2π`, and a declared principal-angle display `(-π,π]`. At zero, the angle is **undefined**, not a physical zero phase. Avoid using `atan(b/a)` without quadrants. Derive angle addition through multiplication. Define `e^(u+iv)=e^u(cos v+i sin v)`, check the exponential law from angle addition, and check the real-parameter ODE `z'=iz,z(0)=1`. This links to the existing real rotation system without circular reliance on transforms. Derive inverse Euler formulas, integer powers and the n distinct roots of a nonzero complex number. Show all fourth roots of 1 in a compact polygon. Optional logarithm depth explains `log r+i(θ+2πk)` and why a principal branch does not preserve every familiar real log identity. Distinguish the principal square root from the two roots of `z²=w`.

### 2. One oscillation is a rotating arrow seen from the side

Write `A cos(2πft+φ)=Re(Ae^(iφ)e^(2πift))`. Define hertz, radians per second `ω=2πf`, period `1/f` for nonzero frequency, peak versus RMS amplitude, and the chosen positive rotation direction. A phasor is the complex amplitude relative to a reference frequency/time origin; it is not the complete time-varying signal. Derivatives multiply a complex exponential by `iω`, rotating by a quarter-turn and scaling by angular frequency. Taking real parts commutes with a real linear differential operator.

Show the same two-tone trace with a changed relative phase: frequency magnitudes remain unchanged while the waveform and value at a given time change. A global time shift changes each frequency's phase proportionally to that frequency; an arbitrary phase edit is not necessarily a single time shift. Derive the conjugate pair for one real cosine. Negative frequency is opposite rotation, not negative elapsed time or a redundant error in an otherwise complex signal. At a zero coefficient, phase carries no defined information; near cancellation, phase can be sensitive even when the reconstructed signal is not.

### 3. Recover the ingredients by multiplying and averaging

On one stated period T, define the normalized complex inner product by averaging `x(t)conj(y(t))`. Derive cancellation of `exp(2πi(k−m)t/T)` over an integer number of periods, including the `k=m` case. Consequently `c_k=(1/T)∫x(t)e^(−2πikt/T)dt` extracts a harmonic coordinate. Work the default example: `c_1=1`, `c_−1=1`, `c_3=i/2`, `c_−3=−i/2`, all others zero; coefficients have volts, and paired magnitudes convert to real peak amplitudes only with the stated DC/Nyquist exceptions later.

Derive the finite orthogonal-projection residual and Pythagorean identity before claiming a best least-squares approximation in the chosen span. Explain both cosine and sine channels: a cosine-only correlation can miss a quarter-cycle-shifted tone. A useful application is synchronous detection of a known frequency: multiply by two reference quadratures and average over whole periods to reject orthogonal components. Develop the exact noiseless calculation; do not claim arbitrary noise is removed or any empirical SNR improvement. Changed finite windows are handled later.

### 4. A Fourier series is a limit with conditions

Calculate the odd-harmonic sine coefficients of a period-1 square wave, with endpoints explicitly assigned. Partial sums use the actual computed coefficients. Derive the coefficient sign and scale, show jump midpoint value, and distinguish pointwise, mean-square and uniform error. State the piecewise-smooth periodic convergence theorem with finite one-sided values and specify behavior at the seam. Explain the Dirichlet-kernel averaging identity and how cancellation away from a point supports convergence; label the complete functional-analysis extension as further depth, not a proof supposedly established by pictures.

Use the square wave's narrow overshoot region to show why adding terms does not make the maximum jump-neighborhood error vanish. The exact asymptotic overshoot, if quoted, must be independently derived/checked through the sine integral and identified as a limit relative to the jump, not a measured percentage of the entire signal. Avoid blanket termwise differentiation: even a convergent series can fail that operation; briefly contrast integrating coefficients and differentiating a discontinuous target. Link Real Analysis54 for the full convergence taxonomy. A smooth/triangle comparison is useful only if its coefficient decay is actually derived, not added as decoration.

### 5. From periodic coordinates to a continuous spectrum

Give a compact representation map: periodic continuous time → discrete Fourier-series harmonics; nonperiodic continuous time → continuous Fourier transform; an infinite sampled sequence → periodic DTFT; N stored values → N DFT coordinates. Fourier series is not the same integral as the transform of an all-time pure sinusoid.

Declare the frequency-in-hertz convention `X(f)=∫ x(t)e^(−2πift)dt` and inverse with `e^(+2πift)` and no `1/(2π)` when integrating over f. The angular-frequency version uses ω and its inverse factor `1/(2π)`. Show a units row: x in V, t in s, X in V·s, df in Hz, reconstructed x in V. Start with a finite rectangular pulse, derive its sinc transform including the removable value at zero, then construct a triangular pulse by convolution; the latter supplies an integrable-transform inversion example. Time widening narrows the frequency profile under a specified amplitude/area convention, so avoid a vague conservation claim.

Give sufficient hypotheses: x integrable defines the ordinary transform; x and X integrable allow ordinary inversion at continuity points. Explain the symmetric limiting interpretation for a piecewise-smooth pulse whose sinc transform is not absolutely integrable. Periodic tones require the already-taught series or an explicitly introduced generalized transform; do not use divergent ordinary integrals and draw them as finite spikes. State Parseval/Plancherel at the proper square-integrable level, deriving the finite version here and identifying the extension rather than hiding it. Derive time shift, modulation, scaling with `|a|`, and the derivative rule with vanishing boundary/integrability conditions. These explain phase delay, frequency translation and filtering; do not offer an unqualified table of recipes.

### 6. N observations have N exact Fourier coordinates

Define a DFT with negative forward exponent and an unscaled forward sum; inverse carries `1/N`. Derive the root-of-unity sum and inverse exactly. Work `[1,2,0,−1] → [2,1−3i,0,1+3i]` through visible per-sample contributions. Both time energy and frequency energy/N equal 6. This is exact finite linear algebra: it reconstructs the samples, not a uniquely known continuous signal. Frequencies are `k f_s/N` with upper bins interpreted as negative frequencies. Even/odd length, DC, and the single even-N Nyquist coefficient must be explicit. Real-input conjugate symmetry does not hold for every complex input. Do not report meaningful phase for a numerically negligible coefficient without disclosing a display threshold.

Derive a radix-two even/odd split `X_k=E_k+w_N^k O_k`, `X_(k+N/2)=E_k−w_N^k O_k` with one small butterfly figure. The exact DFT is unchanged; the algorithm reorganizes arithmetic. Explain `T(N)=2T(N/2)+O(N)` only for the stated radix-two family; arbitrary-length DFTs exist and modern FFT implementations need not require power-of-two input. No fabricated speed plot. Supply an actual direct-DFT implementation, optional small recursive FFT, and a NumPy round-trip comparison with installed-version evidence.

### 7. Samples cannot identify every continuous oscillation

Derive equality of sampled exponentials at f and `f+m f_s`. Keep a signed alias frequency so its phase is correct; when folding a real cosine to a positive frequency, negate phase if the signed representative is negative. Default: `cos(2π·13t+π/3)` and `cos(2π·3t−π/3)` give the same n/16 samples. Draw both continuous curves and the one actual sample set. Changing the sample rate changes acquisition; changing a label cannot repair acquired values.

State an ideal sampling theorem for a bandlimited square-integrable signal and all uniform samples with `f_s>2B`; define normalized sinc reconstruction and distinguish its infinite exact sum from a truncated demonstration. Bandlimit, sample timing, complete sequence and ideal reconstruction matter. At the Nyquist boundary an appropriately phased sine can vanish at every sample, so the safe strict condition is not replaced by an unrestricted equality claim. Anti-alias filtering acts before acquisition/decimation; later filtering cannot distinguish two already identical sampled inputs. Explain bandwidth loss rather than suggesting filtering recovers removed content.

### 8. Finite windows smear frequencies; zero padding fills the display grid

Separate the acquisition spacing `1/f_s`, N observed values, nominal record period `T=N/f_s`, last sample time `(N−1)/f_s`, and the padded transform length M. An off-bin tone over a finite window is not a pure DFT basis vector. Derive its geometric-sum/Dirichlet response with stable removable limits; show multiplication by a time window becomes spectral convolution. Use a 5.5 Hz tone at 64 Hz over N=64, then compare actual longer acquisition N=128 with M=256 zero padding of the original 64 values. Longer observation changes the available data; padding evaluates the same finite-sequence transform at more frequencies.

Compare rectangular and explicitly defined periodic Hann weights. Expose the windowed samples and spectral lobe shape on linked axes. A lower sidelobe can come with a wider main lobe; there is no universal best window. If using amplitude normalization, divide by the actual sum of weights and distinguish coherent bin-centered sinusoidal amplitude from noisy/off-bin estimation. DC and Nyquist do not get indiscriminate factor two. `|X|²` without duration/sample-rate/window scaling is not automatically a physical PSD.

Supply a small explicit power-units bridge so the learner can do more than repeat that warning. With no detrending, the two-sided finite estimate `P_k=|X_w[k]|²/(f_s Σw_n²)` has V²/Hz units. For M transform bins, `ΣP_k(f_s/M)=Σw_n²x_n²/Σw_n²` by the finite Parseval identity; rectangular weights reduce this to the sample mean square. One-sided real spectra merge conjugate bins and keep DC/Nyquist unpaired. This is a finite normalization identity, not a proof of an unbiased or consistent population spectrum estimate. Check the actual SciPy periodogram with `detrend=False` and the exact same window, rather than accidentally comparing against its default mean subtraction. Route full estimator uncertainty and neural interpretation to [the saved spectral note](topic-notes/spectral-and-time-frequency-analysis-of-neural-signals.md). A short moved-window example can illustrate burst timing, but a full STFT interface is not necessary if it duplicates the existing planned owner.

### 9. A filter combines shifted responses

Derive finite convolution from impulses and shifted copies before showing a formula. A small contribution table/slide overlay uses x=`[1,2,0,−1]`, h=`[1,1]`: linear convolution `[1,3,2,−1,−1]`, four-point circular convolution `[0,3,2,−1]`. The last value wraps into the first. Prove the DFT multiplication/circular convolution identity by reindexing and explain padding to at least `N+L−1` for the linear result. The data example is dimensionless discrete arithmetic; a discretized continuous integral needs its time-step factor.

Connect continuous LTI convolution to ODE50's variation of constants. For `y'+a y=a x`, a>0 and zero initial state, the impulse response is `a e^(−at)u(t)`. Derive `H(iω)=a/(a+iω)`, magnitude and phase, and show the default two-tone output for `a=2π s⁻¹`. At 1 Hz the gain is `1/√2`, phase `−π/4`; at 3 Hz gain `1/√10`, phase `−atan(3)`. The actual startup solution is steady response plus the homogeneous correction required by y(0). A frequency-response curve does not contain every initial transient. A nonlinear/time-varying system need not preserve individual sinusoidal frequencies.

### 10. Laplace adds exponential weighting and an explicit region

Write `s=σ+iω`: e^(−σt) changes the weighting envelope, while e^(−iωt) supplies the oscillatory comparison. Introduce unilateral `∫_0∞` for ordinary post-initial functions first. For `e^(−at)` calculate `1/(s+a)` only on `Re(s)>−a`. Compare finite horizon integrals with their proposed infinite limit; on the boundary an oscillating finite integral is not automatically convergent. Show the ROC as a shaded half-plane with a boundary and pole, not a heatmap that assigns a valid transform everywhere.

Then define the bilateral transform. The same expression `1/(s+a)` can come from a right-sided exponential with right-half-plane ROC or a signed left-sided exponential with left-half-plane ROC. A rational formula without ROC/causality is incomplete. Fourier evaluation on the imaginary axis is valid where that axis belongs to the appropriate convergence domain (or under a separately stated generalized/boundary interpretation). A growing causal exponential may have a Laplace transform but no ordinary Fourier transform. Explain complex-domain versus real-frequency input before presenting poles and zeros.

### 11. Initial conditions and shifted inputs survive the algebra

For a locally absolutely continuous ordinary function with suitable exponential bounds, derive `L_+(y')=sY−y(0+)` by integration by parts. Higher derivatives include every initial term. Separately introduce an area-one ideal impulse operationally through integration against a smooth test function; if an impulse occurs at the origin, explicitly use a `0−` generalized convention and pre-jump initial data, or solve the jump and use a post-jump ordinary IVP. Do not mix conventions or double-count an initial jump.

Derive a compact transform table from exponentials, sine/cosine, differentiation in s and finite shifts. Work a nonzero-initial first-order equation, a delayed rectangular input, and a second-order system with a step/impulse after t=0. Partial fractions must include repeated poles and irreducible quadratics; verify the returned function against the differential equation, initial state and jump/delay conditions. `u(t−b)g(t−b)` transforms to `e^(−bs)G(s)`; `u(t−b)g(t)` needs a different shifted expression. A complete worked counterexample prevents rote misuse.

Connect rational poles to modes `t^m e^(pt)` and repeated-root factors. State causal proper rational BIBO stability through the integrable impulse response and pole conditions after genuine input-output cancellation; strictly proper h is an ordinary function, while a proper feedthrough term has a delta component treated separately. Show a hidden state counterexample `A=diag(1,−1), B=(0,1), C=(0,1)`: transfer `1/(s+1)` does not certify internal stability for arbitrary initial states. A narrow final-value branch can derive the rational pole condition from modes and demonstrate the failure on sin t. Full contour inversion is clearly advanced additional study, not something necessary to run the examples.

### 12. Transfer, boundaries and the next step

Optional discrete-time bridge: causal `h[n]=a^n` has `H(z)=1/(1−a/z)` for `|z|>|a|`. Derive the geometric sum and evaluate the unit circle only when allowed; distinguish z from Laplace's s and connect `z=e^(sΔt)` as a mapping of exponential modes, not a universal equality of all continuous/discrete systems. A short derivative-in-frequency/PDE bridge explains why boundary conditions select the basis before using a spectral method.

The capstone keeps the central two-tone/filter question but changes frequencies, relative phases, acquisition rate/window and initial state. The learner must report a complex reconstruction, a correct alias explanation, a window/padding decision, startup versus steady filter output, and a separate Laplace verification with initial data. “A taller peak means a larger physical tone” is not an acceptable answer without normalization and window assumptions. Closing readiness links to Conditioning52: small Fourier residuals, phase near zeros and pole sensitivity are useful reasons to learn error analysis next.

## Native programs and independent practice

Implementation must write complete runnable Python programs, with actual captured stdout, precise package versions and local math-code correspondence. Standard-library programs cover complex arithmetic, phasor synthesis/projection, explicit DFT/inverse, radix-two recombination, alias equality, convolution, finite Laplace integral and partial-fraction verification; NumPy/SciPy programs add FFT conventions, window comparison and independent time-domain/filter checks. The eventual exact number follows the examples, not a quota. Each program has an explicit prediction question visible before the code, setup, input contract, interpretation and changed case. Do not store hidden questions in data that the rendering component omits.

The design checks already confirm product/division, the exact four-point transform/inverse/energy, phase-aware alias samples, circular versus linear outputs, symbolic first-order step response, 41 independent filter convolution integrals and 81 finite weighted Laplace integrals. An initial test compared floating absolute-square energy with exact equality; the harness was corrected to test the exact analytical energy separately and use a stated numerical tolerance for the library calculation. Those archived design checks were **fixture checks only**. The subsequent production-model, actual-program and browser evidence is recorded separately in the author verification; the old numerical file is deliberately unchanged.

Plan changed practice with hints before explained solutions:

1. Compute a changed complex quotient and all cube roots of a non-real value; explain quadrant, principal argument and why there are several roots.
