## 2026-09-11 — Carry equation normalization and solver error into the mesh-convergence argument

- Status: resolved — adapted in Numerical PDE57, author-verified 11 September 2026
- Origin: `conditioning-stability-numerical-analysis`, [design](../CONDITIONING-STABILITY-LESSON-DESIGN.md), now authored with final native/browser evidence in [the verification record](../CONDITIONING-STABILITY-VERIFICATION.md); independent review/integration are separate.
- Destination and ownership rationale: the existing Numerical PDE57 brief owns grid/boundary, heat/Poisson, finite-element and refinement mechanisms and already requires Conditioning52. It should make those specific error conversions concrete; a general numerical-analysis lesson cannot establish the stability of every PDE scheme.
- Idea and learning benefit: distinguish discretization defect from the residual of an incompletely solved discrete system, and show how both enter the final error under an actual mesh-dependent norm and inverse bound. A solver's smaller raw residual is not by itself evidence of better approximation to the continuous problem.
- Existing coverage: destination's exact planned brief covers grid resolution, boundary conditions, stable time step, heat/Poisson, finite elements and known-solution refinement, but has no current body or note explicitly defining the residual/defect normalization. Numerical Methods38 already teaches a nested quadrature/root budget and warns that residual is not solution error. Conditioning52 now teaches the intervening norm/condition/backward-error synthesis.
- Proposed treatment: introduce the discrete operator and physical/grid norm, then derive the chosen scheme's defect, stability and algebraic-solver contribution. Include a manufactured/known solution and a comparison where solver tolerance is tightened relative to a mesh target. Retain separate boundary/truncation/solver units; decide how the requested tolerance scales as h changes. Derive a specific CFL or coercivity condition rather than generalizing it to every method.
- Explanation/example: with `u_h` the restriction of the exact solution, `τ_h=b_h-A_h u_h`, and computed residual `r_h=b_h-A_h xhat_h`, the exact finite identity is `A_h(xhat_h-u_h)=τ_h-r_h`. Hence `||xhat_h-u_h||≤||A_h^-1|| (||τ_h||+||r_h||)` in the declared compatible norms, when invertible. Multiplying all equations by a small factor reduces both raw defects and residuals while increasing the inverse norm, so the resulting error bound must not become artificially stronger. A PDE author must additionally address approximation/restriction error in the quantity actually reported.
- Prerequisites and boundaries: Conditioning52 now teaches a complete exact constant-ODE counterexample to consistency without uniform stability, with error `(2^N-1)/N²` at fixed time. That is not a proof of a PDE equivalence theorem. Numerical PDE57 owns well-posedness and assumptions needed for its own linear or nonlinear convergence claims, plus boundary compatibility and time/space coupling.
- Evidence: independently derived finite algebraic identity and [FNC zero-stability discussion](https://fncbook.com/zerostability/) for the distinction between consistency and amplification, read 11 September 2026. The destination's actual PDE matrices/norms have not yet been implemented or verified; its formulas must be investigated locally.
- Resolution: Numerical PDE57 sections 3–6 derive A(error)=τ−r, the maximum-principle/barrier inverse bound L²/8, scaled-system equivalence and the added interpolation error. The live certificate evaluates the residual of the stored binary64 vector with exact fractions and conservative conversions, rather than treating a small floating residual as rigorous. The changed L=3/2/source2/nonzero-endpoint capstone derives a new physical residual allowance and reports finite-budget failure and success. Heat and conservation sections separately prove the conditions for their actual schemes; no general PDE equivalence theorem is inferred from the origin's ODE example.
