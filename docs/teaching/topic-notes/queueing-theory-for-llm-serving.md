# Authoring notes: Queueing Theory for LLM Serving

Canonical topic ID: `queueing-theory-for-llm-serving`

## 2026-09-11 — Keep mean populations, admission boundaries and latency tails distinct

- **Status:** open.
- **Origin:** mathematics `queueing-theory-m-m-1-m-g-1-little-s-law`; [scoped design](../QUEUEING-THEORY-LESSON-DESIGN.md).
- **Destination and rationale:** the mathematics lesson will teach the conservation and stationary FCFS queue mechanisms. This later lesson owns how those quantities map to queued requests, admitted GPU/KV populations, state-dependent batching and serving SLO measurements. The destination was inspected only in its opening and mathematical-foundation sections during this scope review; this is not a full audit or authorization to rewrite it now.
- **Existing coverage and concrete defects:** `src/learn/data/topics/queueing-theory-llm-serving.jsx` opening Little paragraph says L=200 means 200 requests in flight “at all times.” It is a mean over a specified population/time law, not a pointwise constant. A population including a CPU waiting queue cannot automatically be equated to concurrently allocated GPU KV states. The M/M/1 tail paragraph describes total time but writes `rho * exp(-(mu-lambda)t)`, which is the stationary FCFS queue-wait survival for t>=0. Its stated rho=.9,mu=1 p99≈25.2 fails even substitution into that displayed queue-wait formula.
- **Proposed treatment:** use random total duration T and queue wait Tq, with W=E[T] and Wq=E[Tq] (or another clearly declared notation). Under stable stationary FCFS M/M/1, P(T>t)=exp(-(mu-lambda)t), while P(Tq>t)=rho exp(-(mu-lambda)t), including mass 1-rho at zero wait. At rho=.9,mu=1, total p99≈46.0517 seconds; queue p99≈44.9981 seconds. For low percentiles, queue quantiles can be zero. Reassess downstream formulas, plots, simulated comparisons and practice against the corrected metric, rather than changing one equation in isolation.
- **Learning benefit and representation:** draw an API boundary containing an external waiting lane and a separate admitted/KV lane. Apply Little separately to each legitimate population; explain what each service metric measures. Beside the two survival curves show the zero-wait atom, tail threshold and mean. A useful changed task gives matching average system concurrency but different admitted concurrency, then asks which memory constraint can actually be inferred.
- **Boundaries:** classical FCFS tails are not automatically valid for processor sharing, continuous batching, prefill/decode service dependence or load-dependent effective throughput. A model predicts only under its assumptions. Capacity feasibility, mean occupancy, percentiles and instantaneous maximum occupancy are different statements. Preserve useful existing serving-specific material but verify empirical and hardware claims from their actual primary sources before relying on them.
- **Evidence:** [Sigman's pathwise Little notes](https://www.columbia.edu/~ks20/stochastic-I/stochastic-I-LL.pdf), pp. 1–4, inspected 10 September 2026 UTC; [MIT birth/death notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-203j-logistical-and-transportation-planning-methods-fall-2006/a9235f5e4e0aee12a55778e5beaf0ddb_lec5.pdf), balance and stationary model; the tail identities follow by conditioning on geometric occupancy and independent exponential FCFS stages and must be rederived/verified during authoring. Python direct substitution independently gives `log(.9/.01)/.1 = 44.9980967033` and `-log(.01)/.1 = 46.0517018599`. These are theoretical example values, not measured serving latency.
