- Destination and ownership rationale: GMM is position 16, immediately after Anomaly Detection. It is the natural home for a learned probability density, soft component responsibilities and EM. The preceding topics should preview that connection without teaching the full mixture algorithm out of order.
- Learning benefit: distinguish three questions on the same point: which component best accounts for it, how much density the entire model assigns near it, and whether its score warrants an alert under a declared decision rule. A far-away point can have decisive relative component responsibility and low total density. Use that contrast to connect the new probabilistic mechanism to the previous lesson's separation of score and action.
- Existing coverage: scoped inspection of `src/learn/data/topics/gaussian-mixture-models-gmm-em-algorithm.jsx` introduction, §3.5 and exercises 1, 4–6 found useful density/EM material but several claims needing reassessment. This was a sequence-bridge inspection, not a full review of the unrequested lesson.
- Proposed treatment: introduce responsibilities versus mixture density with a tiny two-component example and two linked views, then reuse the previous lessons' observation IDs, representation and evaluation denominator where appropriate. Introduce any new dataset explicitly. Keep reference labels separate from density fitting and parameter selection; calibrate a downstream alert decision using suitable held-out data rather than presenting an arbitrary training percentile as a general false-alarm guarantee.
- Specific accuracy checks for the destination author:
  - The introduction says the unconstrained GMM likelihood is bounded above and gives a convergence guarantee. Reassess against covariance collapse around an observation; distinguish monotone objective values, boundedness, parameter convergence and a stationary point. Explain the actual constrained/regularized objective used by the teaching implementation.
  - The exercise solution equates `covariance_type='spherical'` plus a responsibility threshold of 0.5 with k-means. The API's spherical option allows a separate variance for each component; a shared isotropic variance, equal weights and explicit hard assignments are different restrictions. Show the hard-assignment correspondence and the small-variance limit precisely, then distinguish both from ordinary soft EM and its library API. For more than two components a 0.5 threshold can leave a point unassigned.
  - The responsibility denominator in the current exercise changes the observation subscript while summing over components. Keep the same observation in every component term. Its suggested denominator clipping also does not generally produce normalized or uniform responsibilities; teach log-sum-exp with a concrete underflow example.
  - The current troubleshooting answer treats every likelihood decrease as a bug and suggests a diagonal covariance addition as a universal remedy. Separate finite-precision tolerance, a changed/approximate optimization objective, singularity and an actual incorrect update before prescribing a fix. A negative log-determinant alone does not mean a covariance matrix is invalid.
- Prerequisites and boundaries: means/covariances, conditional probability, logarithms and the previous three topics' evaluation conventions. Supply a local refresher before the EM derivation. This note does not authorize changing GMM, the catalogue, or the current reader.
