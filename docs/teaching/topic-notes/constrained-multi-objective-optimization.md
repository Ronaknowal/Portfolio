# Authoring notes: Constrained & Multi-Objective Optimization

Canonical topic ID: constrained-multi-objective-optimization

## 2026-09-10 — Continue from projection and proximal steps to coupled constraints

- Status: implemented and verified (10 September 2026)
- Origin: [Convex Optimization design](../CONVEX-OPTIMIZATION-DESIGN.md), currently being implemented.
- Destination and rationale: this lesson is the natural owner of constrained solver choices and coupled feasible sets. Convex Optimization introduces first-order certificates, projection onto a small budget triangle, subgradients, scalar soft thresholding and proximal-gradient basics. KKT/Duality owns multiplier theory; the detailed adaptive-optimizer lesson addresses a different mechanism.
- Idea and benefit: explain when projecting onto the complete feasible set is costly, why composing two individual projections is generally not the projection onto their intersection, and how a justified splitting/augmented-Lagrangian method handles coupling. This connects simple geometry to an actual constrained solver rather than listing names.
- Existing coverage: a scoped case-insensitive search of current curriculum/brief sources and the existing Constrained, KKT and Gradient Descent lesson bodies found no explicit proximal/subgradient/ADMM teaching. Those older bodies still await this rollout's review. This is not a claim that the full catalogue has no incidental occurrence.
- Proposed treatment: retain the destination's multi-objective/Pareto outcomes, establish constraint feasibility and stopping residuals, then assess a sufficiently explained optional projection/splitting branch. A small two-set example should expose the incorrect one-pass composition claim before teaching an appropriate iterative method. An ADMM treatment must explain variables, penalty, primal and dual residuals, assumptions and stopping criteria; a few unexplained update equations do not meet the standard.
- Prerequisites/boundaries: convex sets, gradients, first-order certificates and the proximal definition; distinguish closed convex sets from discrete/nonconvex constraints and a mathematical convergence statement from finite solver behavior. This origin should not become an entire constrained-optimization course.
- Evidence: selected [Parikh/Boyd proximal slides](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/prox_slides.pdf), slides3–6 and17–25 read10 September2026. The overview's broad “always works” language is not an adequate convergence contract; research the exact assumptions before using it. Its dated timing table is not a benchmark for this website.
- Resolution: implemented in the rewritten destination, sections2–5. Both one-pass projection orders are checked against the exact whole-set projection; projected-gradient stationarity, finite penalties/barriers and a complete two-block consensus ADMM solve then develop the mechanism. ADMM variables, fixed penalty, exact subproblems, both residuals, stopping tolerances and the conditional convex convergence statement are explicit. The original multi-objective outcomes remain and are substantially developed. The original discovery and its historical coverage assessment above are retained as context.
- Implementation/verification: [destination design](../CONSTRAINED-MULTIOBJECTIVE-DESIGN.md) and [actual author verification](../CONSTRAINED-MULTIOBJECTIVE-VERIFICATION.md); eleven executed programs, independent constrained/optimality oracles, full1440/390 interactions and1440/390/320 reading/keyboard/visual evidence.
