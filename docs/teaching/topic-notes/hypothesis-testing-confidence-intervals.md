Status: implemented in the authorized scoped rewrite,10 September2026. Stable ID `hypothesis-testing-confidence-intervals`, Math Foundations position17. Origin: `maximum-likelihood-map-estimation`, position16, September2026 authoring.

## Carry the Bernoulli boundary into interval coverage

- Current ownership evidence: the actual existing pilot teaches paired t inference and says a binary success rate can use a proportion interval such as Wilson, but the scoped source search found no worked all-success/all-failure or binomial coverage investigation. This is a specific observed gap, not an audit of all statistics lessons.
- Learning benefit: the revised MLE lesson distinguishes fixed-data likelihood, a parameter posterior and a repeated-sample distribution. It derives Var(p-hat)=p(1-p)/n and shows why the plug-in normal standard error returns zero for an all-success or all-failure fit despite uncertainty after a finite study. The next lesson should finish that question through an actual interval procedure rather than leaving a warning without a method.
- Suggested treatment: compare the ordinary Wald proportion interval with a score/Wilson interval on boundary and small-sample data, explaining interval construction/inversion before code. If an exact equal-tailed binomial interval helps, distinguish conservative discrete coverage from exact equality to nominal confidence. Enumerate k=0,…,n under a stated true p to compute actual finite coverage; a simulation can supplement this but is not needed to invent empirical data. Choose the scope and representation according to the new lesson's flow.
- Boundary: do not claim Wilson coverage is always at least nominal, do not call a confidence interval a posterior probability interval, and do not present p-hat=0 or1 as zero epistemic uncertainty. Keep the original effective t-test/interval investigations while deciding how the binary case fits.
- Sources to reassess: [NIST/SEMATECH proportion confidence intervals](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm), inspected for its Wilson/test-inversion and exact-binomial sections; [Geyer's likelihood inference notes](https://www.stat.umn.edu/geyer/f21/5421/notes/likelihood.html), inspected for Wald approximation and regularity caveats. Independently derive/verify the selected implementation and finite coverage rather than accepting broad summary language uncritically.
