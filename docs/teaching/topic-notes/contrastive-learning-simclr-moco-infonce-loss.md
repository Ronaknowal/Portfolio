## 2026-09-10 — Separate the population InfoNCE bound from a trained minibatch score

- Status: open
- Origin: [Mutual Information & Information Bottleneck design](../MUTUAL-INFORMATION-LESSON-DESIGN.md), section 9 of the implemented MI lesson.
- Destination and ownership rationale: the contrastive-learning lesson owns the positive/negative construction, critic, loss, training and transfer evaluation. MI owns the joint-distribution meaning and a short qualified bridge; duplicating a complete contrastive trainer there would obscure its finite bottleneck mechanism.
- Existing coverage: exact topic inventory says planned, individual design required, and no existing destination note. Scoped body/manifest lookup found no published body for this stable ID. This is not a claim that no other lesson mentions contrastive losses.
- Idea and learning benefit: learners should derive the candidate-index classification posterior from one conditional positive and independently sampled marginal negatives, then distinguish the population expected loss from a finite minibatch value and a data-selected critic. They should understand why log K minus the loss is capped by log K without treating this as a ceiling on true MI.
- Proposed treatment: place after a concrete paired-example/negative-sampling trace and before model comparison. Use an exactly enumerable finite joint law to compare true MI with the expected InfoNCE bound at different K and critic choices. A changed negative proposal should visibly break the original density-ratio derivation rather than silently preserve its interpretation. Follow with an independently evaluated trained representation, not just the loss used to fit it.
- Explanation/example: start from a binary noisy channel already taught in MI. Enumerate all K-candidate sets for small K, compare an exact density-ratio critic and a deliberately mismatched positive critic, then sample minibatches to show their variation around the population expectation. Explain duplicated/false negatives and batch dependence in the appropriate method's actual sampling model; do not label every practical loss an exact MI estimator.
- Prerequisites and boundaries: joint/conditional laws, cross-entropy/KL, MI/DPI, expectations and sample variation; derive required neural shapes locally. SimCLR augmentations and MoCo queues need their own sampling/optimization contracts. Do not imply that higher estimated MI universally produces better downstream semantic representations.
- Evidence: van den Oord, Li and Vinyals, [Representation Learning with Contrastive Predictive Coding](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.03748v2), section 2.3, equations 4–5 and stated population bound, read 2026-09-10. McAllester and Stratos, [Formal Limitations on the Measurement of Mutual Information](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/mcallester20a/mcallester20a.pdf), theorem 1.1 and introduction, read the same date: scope its distribution-free high-confidence restrictions rather than claiming every estimator/model has a universal log-N ceiling. The proposed enumerated contrastive lab has not been implemented or verified by the MI author.
- Resolution: not yet reviewed by the destination author. Reassess the current source and pedagogy before implementing, adapting or rejecting the proposal.
