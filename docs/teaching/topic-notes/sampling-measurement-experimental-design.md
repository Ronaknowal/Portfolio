## 2026-09-11 — Carry covariance into the choice of independent measurement units

- Status: resolved — adapted in the destination implementation and author-verified; independent/production review remains separate
- Origin: `random-variables-expectation-covariance`, [current design](../RANDOM-VARIABLES-LESSON-DESIGN.md), scoped Mathematics48 authoring. The destination is Mathematics49, the next actual topic in this module.
- Destination and ownership rationale: Random Variables48 teaches the exact covariance algebra and distinguishes population moments from sample statistics. Sampling49 is the stronger home for deciding what is an independent sampling unit, whether repeated measurements or clusters share errors, and which data collection/assignment design supports the intended inference. Concentration and Hypothesis Testing provide later theorem/inference depth rather than replace these design decisions.
- Existing coverage at note creation: the then-planned Sampling49 brief said repeated measurements are not automatically independent subjects, but there was no published body or prior destination note. Probability15 briefly derives finite-population covariance; Concentration19 has an explicit independent-evidence contract. The current source check does not establish full cluster or paired-design instruction elsewhere.
- Idea and learning benefit: turn the covariance term from a formula into a design question. Learners should identify which observations share a source of variation, explain why more copies do not create more independent evidence, and distinguish paired differences, repeated measurements and independent units before choosing a standard error.
- Proposed treatment: after defining population/unit/measurement and before a sample-size or randomization recommendation, show a small set of units with repeated readings. Label subject-specific or shared systematic error separately from per-reading error. Compare averaging within a unit, collecting a new unit and taking a calibrated paired difference; identify the estimand in each case. Make finite-population without-replacement dependence explicit when relevant.
- Explanation/example: the author-frozen Mathematics48 implements the original synthetic A=10+S+e_A and B=20+S+e_B example, with S equally likely±2 and independent e_A,e_B equally likely±1. Its verified covariance algebra shows Var(A)=Var(B)=5, Cov(A,B)=4, Var((A+B)/2)=9/2 and Var(B−A)=2. These are distributions of different quantities with different means, not automatically competing estimators of the same target. Sampling49 should decide what must be calibrated/subtracted to compare a common target, and how the shared term changes under genuinely new units. The final implemented origin and its evidence were reassessed during destination authoring.
- Prerequisites and boundaries: expectation/variance/covariance, a paired joint outcome and a declared data-generating model. The ordinary n−1 covariance correction assumes iid paired observations with finite second moments; it does not correct arbitrary weights, selection bias or cluster dependence. Do not offer an unsupported universal effective-sample-size formula or a clinical/real-device performance claim.
- Evidence: [MIT18.05 covariance reading](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-05-introduction-to-probability-and-statistics-spring-2022/mit18_05_s22_class07-prep-b.pdf), selected finite covariance/sum-variance properties inspected11September2026.
