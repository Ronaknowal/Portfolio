## 2026-09-10 — Distinguish approximation error from Monte Carlo computation error

- Status: included and adapted in the scoped Variational Inference rewrite; author implementation/native/browser verification passed, user acceptance remains pending.
- Origin: [Monte Carlo/MCMC lesson design](../MONTE-CARLO-MCMC-LESSON-DESIGN.md), sections2,8–9; exact next module owner is `variational-inference` (Math21).
- Actual destination inspected: the currently published body already teaches the mean-field family, ELBO identity, reverse-KL mode preference, a two-normal-mixture quadrature example, reparameterization, minibatches, initialization and comparison with MCMC. Preserve these useful mechanisms rather than treating VI as missing.
- Proposed connection: use one independently tractable target to separate variational-family error, optimization error and Monte Carlo error in an estimated objective/gradient/reported expectation. More samples from a fixed wrong family reduce numerical noise but do not make the family express missing correlations or modes. MCMC MCSE and optimization stopping are different contracts; good diagnostics are not a universal target-accuracy certificate for either method.
- Concrete wording needing assessment: current section3 says directly minimizing distance to the posterior “would require the unknown evidence.” For a fixed model/data, the `log p(D)` term in `KL(q || p(theta|D))` is constant in variational parameters, so it can be dropped for optimization; ELBO expresses that equivalent computable objective. Explain this algebra rather than implying the unknown normalizer prevents every direct KL-gradient calculation. Current section6 says ELBOs of different approximation families are difficult to compare; exact ELBOs for the same normalized joint model/data are comparable, while stochastic estimation error, omitted constants or changed models/data require qualifications.
- Prerequisites/evidence: the origin derives ordinary versus self-normalized importance weighting and distinguishes measured functions/MCSE, including a finite three-state counterexample for ratio bias. The destination should independently verify its ELBO/reverse-KL derivation and gradient estimators against primary VI references, including assumptions for interchanging derivative and expectation. This note does not assert that an unimplemented estimator has been checked.
- Resolution: sections2 and8 now derive the fixed-joint constant and same-model ELBO comparability explicitly. The finite mass/objective investigation separates the exact restricted-family gap from excess optimization error; the coordinate and gradient investigations distinguish family, optimization and numerical errors with independent references. No generic extra error lab was added. The normalized target gradient example also refutes a universal variance ranking, with explicit normalization and estimator assumptions. See [design](../VARIATIONAL-INFERENCE-LESSON-DESIGN.md), [verification](../VARIATIONAL-INFERENCE-VERIFICATION.md), and `scripts/verify-variational-inference-{models,examples}.mjs`. The old three mixture results and useful original coverage are preserved.
