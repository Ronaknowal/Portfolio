## 2026-09-10 — Qualify support-vector sparsity under changed data

- Status: open
- Origin: [Convex Duality & Lagrangian Methods design](../CONVEX-DUALITY-KKT-DESIGN.md), section 8 of its rewritten lesson. The exact destination was resolved with the topic-plan command; no previous destination note existed.
- Destination and ownership rationale: the SVM topic owns training geometry, soft/hard margins and changes to individual observations. The duality lesson derives a complete hard-margin primal/dual example and briefly states the correct certificate condition, but does not expand classifier training/evaluation into a second lesson.
- Idea and learning benefit: let the learner predict when a currently zero-coefficient point can move without changing the existing optimum, and when moving it invalidates the current margin certificate. Distinguish positive coefficient, tight margin, strict slack, and uniqueness.
- Existing coverage: the published SVM source at `src/learn/data/topics/support-vector-machines-svm.jsx:47` says “Move any non-support-vector point and the hyperplane does not change.” This is too broad. The opening passage also speaks of identical retraining after deleting non-support vectors; its formulation, certificate and uniqueness assumptions should be stated. Only the opening passage and nearby KKT/application coverage were inspected for this discovery; this note does not claim a complete SVM audit.
- Proposed treatment: adapt the existing margin investigation to moving an outer point while retaining its label. First keep it strictly outside the current margin; then move it inside. Compare the current certificate with a newly optimized classifier. Explain that positive α implies a tight hard margin, but a tight margin can have zero α in degenerate cases. Keep soft-margin C, slack, feature scaling and bias recovery conventions explicit in their own flow.
- Explanation/example: in one dimension, use negatives at −2,−1 and positives at 1,2. The hard-margin optimum is w=1, β=0. Moving the positive point at 2 to 3 preserves the old certificate with its coefficient zero. Moving it to .5 makes the old margin only .5 and violates the constraint; the new closest opposing pair is −1 and .5, yielding w=4/3 and β=1/3. The decision boundary moves from 0 to −1/4. These values follow by solving both tight margin equations; they are an original teaching scenario, not empirical performance claims.
- Prerequisites and boundaries: separating hyperplanes, signed margin, linear separability, convex KKT and complementarity. Include both classes and an attainable hard-margin problem. Do not equate all boundary points with necessarily positive coefficients. Removing zero-coefficient constraints preserves an existing primal/dual certificate, but uniqueness and which returned parameters remain fixed require separate arguments.
