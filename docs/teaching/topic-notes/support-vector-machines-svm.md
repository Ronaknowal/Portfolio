## 2026-09-10 — Qualify support-vector sparsity under changed data

- Status: implemented and author-verified on 11 September 2026; independent review is separate
- Origin: [Convex Duality & Lagrangian Methods design](../CONVEX-DUALITY-KKT-DESIGN.md), section 8 of its rewritten lesson. The exact destination was resolved with the topic-plan command; no previous destination note existed.
- Destination and ownership rationale: the SVM topic owns training geometry, soft/hard margins and changes to individual observations. The duality lesson derives a complete hard-margin primal/dual example and briefly states the correct certificate condition, but does not expand classifier training/evaluation into a second lesson.
- Idea and learning benefit: let the learner predict when a currently zero-coefficient point can move without changing the existing optimum, and when moving it invalidates the current margin certificate. Distinguish positive coefficient, tight margin, strict slack, and uniqueness.
- Coverage at discovery (archived original): the published SVM source at `src/learn/data/topics/support-vector-machines-svm.jsx:47` says “Move any non-support-vector point and the hyperplane does not change.” This is too broad. The opening passage also speaks of identical retraining after deleting non-support vectors; its formulation, certificate and uniqueness assumptions should be stated. Only the opening passage and nearby KKT/application coverage were inspected for this discovery; this note does not claim a complete SVM audit.
- Proposed treatment: adapt the existing margin investigation to moving an outer point while retaining its label. First keep it strictly outside the current margin; then move it inside. Compare the current certificate with a newly optimized classifier. Explain that positive α implies a tight hard margin, but a tight margin can have zero α in degenerate cases. Keep soft-margin C, slack, feature scaling and bias recovery conventions explicit in their own flow.
- Explanation/example: in one dimension, use negatives at −2,−1 and positives at 1,2. The hard-margin optimum is w=1, β=0. Moving the positive point at 2 to 3 preserves the old certificate with its coefficient zero. Moving it to .5 makes the old margin only .5 and violates the constraint; the new closest opposing pair is −1 and .5, yielding w=4/3 and β=1/3. The decision boundary moves from 0 to −1/4. These values follow by solving both tight margin equations; they are an original teaching scenario, not empirical performance claims.
- Prerequisites and boundaries: separating hyperplanes, signed margin, linear separability, convex KKT and complementarity. Include both classes and an attainable hard-margin problem. Do not equate all boundary points with necessarily positive coefficients. Removing zero-coefficient constraints preserves an existing primal/dual certificate, but uniqueness and which returned parameters remain fixed require separate arguments.
- Evidence: the scoped local source passage above; [scikit-learn's mathematical formulation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/svm.html#mathematical-formulation), read 10 September 2026 at documentation version 1.9.1, particularly its primal/dual soft-margin equations and kernel Gram constraints; the originating lesson's independently solved hard-margin CVXPY example and KKT proof. The new moved-point scenario is analytically derived here; a future author must execute and visually verify its chosen implementation.
- Resolution: implemented in sections 2 and 4. The movable-point certificate, independently optimized changed positions, duplicate coefficient program, KKT table and uniqueness proof distinguish old feasibility from arbitrary movement. Actual desktop/phone views and keyboard controls passed; see the final destination verification below.

## 2026-09-11 — Separate kernel validity, dual curvature and uniqueness

- Status: implemented and author-verified on 11 September 2026; independent review is separate
- Origin: [Functional Analysis & RKHS design](../FUNCTIONAL-ANALYSIS-RKHS-LESSON-DESIGN.md), scoped kernel-section/practice inspection.
- Coverage at discovery (archived original): the published SVM kernel section identifies symmetric finite-Gram positivity with a Mercer condition and says it ensures a unique convex dual solution. The actual general condition is positive semidefiniteness for every finite input list; Mercer integral expansions impose additional conditions. PSD gives a concave quadratic dual maximization objective, but does not by itself give strict concavity or unique coefficients. Duplicate feature vectors can create null coefficient directions.
- Proposed treatment: distinguish unique separating function/weight vector, intercept and dual coefficients. Work a duplicated-point example and show which predictions remain fixed despite equivalent dual coefficients. Preserve the learner's kernel geometry, while stating the correct existence and rank conditions. A finite-C soft-margin dual has bounded box constraints, so an indefinite Hessian alone does not make that bounded feasible problem unbounded.
- Ownership: full constrained SVM degeneracy belongs here; RKHS supplies general projection and kernel-ridge rank distinctions. This is not an audit of every SVM claim.
- Evidence: the actual SVM section 3.4 and final kernel exercise; [Schölkopf et al. 2001, kernel construction and representer theorem](https://alex.smola.org/papers/2001/SchHerSmo01.pdf), read 10 September UTC; [Kanagawa et al. 2018, theorem4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02582), for the conditioned Mercer statement. Future author must execute the chosen SVM example and inspect final prose/UI.
- Resolution: implemented in sections 4–5: finite Gram witness and duplicate comparison, local feature construction, PSD versus strict concavity, unique feature weight versus potentially nonunique bias/alpha, and compact finite-C boundedness. The actual XOR models have independent constrained optimization checks; kernel practice includes a changed indefinite matrix.
