## Intended teaching progression

1. A square map may move most directions but preserve certain lines. Define nonzero eigenvector, signed eigenvalue and zero output carefully, after an explicit hand multiplication.
2. Find the scalar first: (A−λI)v=0 requires singularity; derive the 2×2 determinant equation and then its null space. Trace/product checks supplement, not replace, pair verification.
3. Assemble independent eigenvectors into a basis; derive AS=SΛ, A=SΛS⁻¹ and A^k=SΛ^kS⁻¹. Real symmetric spectral theorem, distinct-eigenvalue orthogonality proof, repeated eigenspaces and a defective shear distinguish conditions.
4. Repeated updates reveal growth, decay, flips and rotations. Eigenvalues describe eventual linear behavior; nonnormal matrices can grow transiently even with spectral radius below one. Unit-modulus eigenvalues require Jordan/semisimplicity care. Continuous-time real-part criterion is a clearly separated deeper bridge.
5. Derive variance along a unit direction from a centered four-point dataset, maximize a weighted eigenvalue sum, reconstruct projected points and check the SVD relationship. Explain scale/units, noise and repeated-subspace interpretation rather than declaring all leading components meaningful.
6. Give non-PCA uses with worked values: mixing two compartments (column-stochastic convention and stationary state), and coupled oscillation modes as a deeper generalized-eigenproblem bridge with units/assumptions. Avoid unsupported empirical/clinical claims.
7. Numerical use: appropriate eig/eigh, residual and symmetry checks, nonunique signs, conditioning/gaps, normalized power iteration with strict dominant-magnitude/start assumptions and a deliberately failing start. Characteristic polynomials are a hand-analysis tool, not a recommended dense numerical implementation.
8. Independent calculation, counterexample diagnosis, variance-budget and changed-update tasks with hints/solutions. Connect local linearization to the next Jacobian lesson.
