The five characteristic caveats named in the review brief were checked sentence by sentence and are all present, each with a single clear home:

- **A score used to choose a setting is not an estimate of that setting's performance.** The lesson-opening `Callout` (`.jsx` L68–74) is a genuine single home: it names *selection score* against *assessment* and states that the two "are never averaged together, never plotted on the same axis, and never quoted interchangeably — including inside the investigations, where the two always occupy separate rows." I verified that promise holds everywhere a number is printed: §6's fold mean and pooled accuracy average only protected results; the explorer's `<dl>` puts "Selection score (chose the setting)" and "Protected result (assessed the procedure)" on separate rows; `NestedLab` does the same at Step 3 and Step 5; and `validation-models.js` enforces it at the type level — `selectionScore` and `assessedAccuracy` never share a field name (L396–403), with the comments "The inner mean that chose k. Selection evidence, not assessment" and "Produced by rows that were protected from the choice above."
- **Fold variance is not a standard error of a population mean.** `.jsx` L511–518, with the general covariance formula, the equal-correlation simplification presented explicitly as an assumption, the "mean ± 1.96 fold standard error" warning, and the Bengio–Grandvalet citation stated accurately as ruling out a *universal* unbiased estimator rather than all uncertainty analysis. Reinforced by the blueprint misconception list. The specification's "No fold-spread CI" is honoured: no interval is drawn anywhere in the lesson.
- **Grouping and stratification change what the estimate means.** `.jsx` L205–211 ("a practical allocation choice, not a universal statistical correction", with the two-positives/five-folds impossibility), `.jsx` L232–240 ("Calling that automatically wrong would change the question"; "A splitter name is not a substitute for writing the required boundary"), and `.jsx` L472–478 ("Stratified and structured splits introduce additional conditions; do not transfer this simple independent-sampling statement to every design without checking them").

### A4. Model and lab logic read for defects

- `foldPlan` (`validation-models.js` L77–150) refuses an empty fold, a duplicate row, an unknown ID and a non-partition when `requirePartition` is on, and reports grouping leaks and per-fold class counts rather than silently repairing anything. `outOfFoldCoverage` (L155–165) returns `'no held-out prediction'` for rows a plan never holds out — the explicit absence F2 needs, never a zero.
- `nearestNeighborRun` (L178–236) resolves distance ties to the smallest source ID by filtering `contributions`, which is in `fold.training` order and therefore row order. I forced a genuine tie in the live lab (row 0 made equidistant from rows 3 and 4) and the page reported "tied with row 4 and resolved to the smallest source ID", predicting from row 3. It also returns `weightedFoldMean`, which the lab prints beside the pooled value as the identity's own check.
- `nestedTrace` (L350–406) validates that neighbour counts are odd (so a binary vote cannot tie) and that k does not exceed the inner training size, and selects via `mean === bestMean` with `find`, which is the first-in-order rule. Its ordering `(distance) || (position)` reproduces the author script's `np.argsort(..., kind="stable")` exactly — verified against my own Python on four fixtures.
- `successiveHalving` (L511–590) keeps `observedUpTo` per candidate, so the plot can draw a trajectory only as far as the schedule paid to look; `keep = Math.max(1, Math.ceil(active.length / factor))` matches the specification; `nominal` and `resumable` accumulate separately and reproduce 150/110 and 270/210 exactly.
- `useInvestigation` (`ValidationShared.jsx` L146–176) is the strongest part of the design. `commit` freezes the prediction *together with a copy of the inputs*; `apply` computes `answerFor(committed.inputs)` rather than from the live fields; any `edit` retires both. Confirmed live: switching I3's outer fold after applying retires the result and shows the "any change retires a recorded prediction" notice, and re-applying grades the new target correctly. The stale-ID crash the design record admits finding is genuinely gone — `protectedRowOf` (`ValidationLabs.jsx` L383) resolves the target the same way in the prompt and in the answer.
- **`Prediction` (L191–253) starts with no radio selected, disables Commit until every question is answered, disables Apply until Commit, and offers no "calculate without predicting" escape.** The sibling reviews found investigations that display or grade before commitment; this one does not. I audited every computed surface in all four labs on first paint, before any interaction: `[data-cv-result]` 0, `.cv-verdict` 0, `[data-cv-mechanism]` 0, `[data-cv-hindsight]` 0, result tables 0, radios checked 0, and each lab showing "Nothing is computed yet. Commit a prediction to bind it to the inputs now in the fields." Everything visible before commitment is *input* — the fold assignment, the candidate patterns, the row roles, the authored trajectories — which is what each specification explicitly requires so the prediction can be informed rather than guessed. The intro's promise at `.jsx` L58–59 is kept exactly.
- `NumberField` (L92–119) publishes only valid values and keeps an invalid draft on screen with an explanation. Live: entering 999 gives "Keep it between -20 and 30"; entering 0.005 gives "Use at most 2 decimal places"; the model keeps its last good state in both cases, and Commit is disabled while a draft is invalid.
- Defects found by reading and then confirmed live: the unreachable previous-trial comparison (**S1**) and the pre-emptive hindsight disclosure (**S4**). No page error and no failed request appeared in any session. Every export of `validation-models.js` is consumed by the page — there are no dead exports, which is unusual for this series and worth noting positively.

### Should-fix

**S1. The "previous trial, kept as a labelled comparison" panel can never render. It is dead code, and with it the specification's comparison requirement.**

Locations: `ValidationShared.jsx` L152 (`previous` state), L154 (`retire`), L165 (`if (result) setPrevious(...)`), L229–232 (the buttons), L247–251 (the panel).

Evidence: `apply` sets `previous` only when a `result` already exists. But `Prediction` disables **Commit** whenever `Boolean(shown)` and **Apply** whenever `Boolean(shown)`, so a second `apply` is impossible while a result is on screen. The only ways forward are `edit` — which calls `retire()`, setting `result` to `null` — or `reset`/`load`, which clear `previous` outright. The guard therefore never fires. I confirmed this on the live page: across every session, in all four labs, including a deliberate apply → load-a-different-preset → commit → apply cycle in I2 and an apply → switch-fold → commit → apply cycle in I3, `[data-cv-previous]` was present **zero** times.

Why it matters: `visual-specifications.md`'s interaction contract requires "Keep a previous trial only as a labeled comparison", and the panel's own text is exactly the right idea — "Those were different inputs, and they are no longer on screen; the result above belongs to the inputs applied now." As shipped, a learner who changes one label and re-runs loses the previous answer entirely, which is precisely the comparison every contrast preset exists to invite.

Fix (small, and it does not touch any number): capture the outgoing result into `previous` at the moment of retirement rather than at the moment of re-application — in `edit`, stash the current `result` before clearing it — and leave `reset` and `load` clearing it as they already do.

**S3. The Optuna run is published honestly but under-explained: its search space contains the grid's winner, on identical folds, at a strictly higher score.**

Locations: `.jsx` L595–611; `validation-examples.js` L22–28.

What the page gets right, and it is most of the way there. The content-phase caveat survives verbatim — "checked against the current API documentation but **not executed during the content phase**, and no exact best settings or scores were invented for it there". The specification authorises phase two to execute the program. **On the narrow questions the brief asks — is it presented honestly, did the content-phase caveat survive, could a reader mistake one pinned run for a property of the method — the answers are yes, yes, and unlikely.**

What is missing is the most instructive fact, which I verified directly. The study's objective scores candidates on `StratifiedKFold(n_splits=3, shuffle=True, random_state=73)` over all 344 rows — **the same three folds the final grid search uses**. A reader who notices that 0.9884058 < 0.9913043 — and the numbers are two sections apart, both prominent — is left to invent an explanation.
