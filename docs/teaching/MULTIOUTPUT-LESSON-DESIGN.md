This is not a promise of every structured-prediction algorithm or production system.

## First-pass learning flow and examples

The continuing core scenario is a small collection of workshop/support messages tagged **code**, **hardware**, and **energy**. A row is one complete message at a declared request time; tags are properties of that same row. A second deliberate scenario predicts two instrument outputs, a temperature change in °C and energy in Wh, to expose the distinct continuous loss/units question. Explain the dataset switch and keep each scenario consistent thereafter.

1. **One input, several questions.** Read one message and choose several tags. Contrast a single exclusive category, a binary tag vector, several categorical attributes and two real-valued measurements. Introduce `(n,d)` input and `(n,L)` target shapes with actual rows; empty label sets may be valid. Independent choices are not independent random variables. Give a complete target-encoding program, stable label vocabulary, duplicate set handling and an explicit unknown-label check.
2. **Freeze what the model can know.** Define observation/request unit, labels and unknown annotation, train/validation/test purposes, group/time split when messages share a conversation or source, and train-only TF-IDF/scaling/label vocabularies. Baselines: per-label training prevalence/majority, most common training vector for subset accuracy, and per-output training means. A complete row stays in one split; splitting label columns or related copies across train/test leaks the task. Rare label support must be reported; stratifying every label combination is not always possible.
3. **Read errors in two directions.** Work through the corrected four-row/three-label fixture before symbols. Define TP/FP/FN/TN, per-cell Hamming, exact-set accuracy, label-wise F1, micro/macro/sample averages and Jaccard. Declare zero-division=0 for both-empty F1/Jaccard in this lesson, while recognizing an alternative empty-set convention. With missing annotations, report observed cells and complete-row subset metrics separately; do not pretend complete-case data represents the full population. Show the exact averaged Jaccard/F1 ranking reversal.
4. **Fit one probability per label.** Refresh sigmoid and stable BCE `logaddexp(0,z)-y*z`; derive gradient `X.T@(P-Y)/n` and independent column objectives. Sum versus mean over labels changes a learning-rate convention; state the chosen objective. Show the original generated fixture in a full runnable NumPy baseline with the same seed, actual output and a train-only constant baseline. A matrix implementation can share a computation without asserting labels are independent.
5. **Which decision does a probability justify?** Derive per-label expected error `p` versus `1-p`, cost threshold, and vector Hamming risk by linearity of expectation without a joint-independence premise. The four-outcome example below makes marginal prediction, a greedy chain and the joint mode visibly different. For subset 0–1 loss use the largest joint mass. For other set-level objectives, state the expected-loss sum; F1 needs more than independent threshold folklore. These are decisions under a known toy distribution, distinct from estimates fitted on finite data.
6. **Follow a chain without confusing it with a joint answer.** Derive chain rule in either label order from the same four masses. Multiply branch probabilities to leaves. Explain zero-probability histories, exact exhaustive decoding, greedy approximation, teacher forcing and inference using predictions. Give complete small exact-PCC code and an actual sklearn trace whose `predict_proba` matches each fitted head on the generated path; it need not equal the marginal obtained by summing all paths. `cv=None` uses true earlier training targets; OOF features require appropriate group/time splitting and train-only transforms inside folds. An ensemble requires several fitted chains and a stated probability/vote aggregation, not one random ordering.
7. **Co-occurrence is a question, not a model-selection oracle.** Count `P(column=1 | row=1)` with its support and compare column prevalence. Then show a balanced mixture of two feature strata, each with independent tag probabilities .1 or .9: pooled both=.41, pooled conditional=.82, but within-stratum independence. A good feature-based marginal model can already explain this association. Chains may improve a restricted fitted model, but deterministic previous predictions are not new observed information beyond X. Don't interpret arrows in prediction order as causal arrows.
8. **Choose thresholds on validation.** Build actual finite threshold regions from sorted distinct scores, including none/all and a stated `>=` tie rule. Show precision/recall/F1 steps while scores remain unchanged. Compare a shared threshold, per-label macro-F1 optimum and a jointly enumerated small micro-F1 search. Report tiny-cohort overfitting risk, rare-label counts and held-out fixed settings. Derive positive-weight BCE optimum `q=w+ p/(w+ p+w−(1−p))`; an imbalanced but calibrated score needs no balance assumption. Handle labels with one training class explicitly.
9. **Represent a whole label set.** Encode exact row patterns, fit a multiclass label-powerset classifier and decode its observed vocabulary; demonstrate a genuinely unseen combination. Show overlapping small labelsets and vote denominators, coverage and ties; name disjoint variant separately. Explain feasible combinations/hierarchy with a small exhaustive risk comparison and declare whether supplied marginal scores are coherent with constraints. Missing truth is not a negative; a masked objective targets observed annotations unless missingness assumptions justify more.
10. **Predict several numbers, not just several bits.** Prove conditional means minimize coordinate squared error; define original-unit MAE/RMSE per output before an explicit scaled weighted aggregate. Independent multi-output least squares solves each target column; residual correlation alone does not make a joint estimator better. Derive `X.T X B=X.T Y` with an optional ridge solve and explain pseudoinverse/rank assumptions locally. Compare vector-native and wrapper estimators with their actual shapes. Several categorical outputs have their own class vocabularies/probability arrays; include a complete `MultiOutputClassifier` example.
11. **Decide what to share.** A common tree split must serve both outputs: exact four-row temperature/energy fixture below changes its chosen split when the declared target scale changes; independent stumps use separate splits. Explain positive transfer, negative transfer and validation, not intrinsic benefit of correlation. A regressor chain uses predicted earlier numbers at inference, not true held-out values; with linear base learners composed predictions remain linear in supplied X. Optional shared-sparsity penalty groups each feature's coefficients across targets; derive a single orthonormal-design row shrinkage and compare the actual library result. Shared features, shared splits, shared shrinkage and a joint distribution are different assumptions.
12. **Compress carefully; scale by measured work.** Optional train-only centered label projection `Z=(Y-mean) V_k`, regression in compressed space, reconstruction and threshold. Explain centered variant versus the cited early uncentered PLST convention. A small rare-label direction can vanish despite high retained energy. Sparse recovery has additional sensing/sparsity assumptions, not simply a low-dimensional manifold claim. Linear BR stores about `L(d+1)` coefficients; a dense linear chain adds `L(L−1)/2`, with sequential within-chain inference. These are operation/storage models, not fabricated benchmarks. Extreme label retrieval needs candidate recall and long-tail evaluation; hierarchical softmax is not automatically a multilabel distribution.
13. **Run a complete changed report.** Provide a literal meaningful multi-tag text corpus with source-group split, train-only vocabulary/TF-IDF, prevalence baseline, independent logistic heads, several chain orders or LP, validation selection and one held-out report. State support, thresholds, zero rules, per-label and aggregate metrics, errors and limits. No synthetic noisy tags presented as real annotation. A second compact continuous report uses train-only target scales and held-out per-output errors. The learner changes a validation cost/group or target scale, freezes again and explains the checked result; no expected model winner is promised.
14. **Practice and move on.** Final independently changed calculations, diagnosis, implementation repair and report tasks with optional hints and separate explained solutions. Include schema, probabilistic action, chain order/path probability, averaging reversal, a missing annotation, threshold ties, weighted score, shared split, unknown combination and a changed capstone. Bridge event-time/censoring data to the actual next lesson.

## Bounded verification and retention

### Research refinement during design

The actual Hsu et al. sections2–3.1 were read after the initial ledger: the analyzed recovery target is a sparse (or approximately sparse under its bounds) conditional mean `E[Y|X=x]`, not merely a sparse individual sampled label vector. A single observed positive chosen uniformly among L labels has a dense conditional mean `(1/L,...,1/L)`. Include this short counterexample in the optional compression bridge; do not promise a recovery theorem from row sparsity alone. The lesson will not reproduce the full recovery theorem. Jesse Read's2015 slides1–5,84–96 and108–116 were inspected: they give useful task/chain geometry and model-class examples, but abbreviated dependence/performance and metric labels are not copied as unconditional claims.
