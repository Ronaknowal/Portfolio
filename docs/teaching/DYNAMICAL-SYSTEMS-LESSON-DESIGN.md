## Learning route and concrete anchors

1. **What information determines the next change?** A cart at position0 moving right and one at position0 moving left share a position but not a state. Explain `(q,v)`, fixed parameters, external input and elapsed time. For cooling `x'=−kx`, x is temperature relative to the environment and k has inverse-time units; x(0)=8,k=.5 gives x(2)=8/e. Contrast this exact flow with an hourly update rule. Smooth local dynamics give unique local trajectories; `x'=x²,x(0)=1` blows up at t=1, so a deterministic formula is not a global existence guarantee. Unique autonomous trajectories cannot cross in full state space; projections can cross.
2. **Does a perturbation stay near, return, or grow?** Define Lyapunov stability as staying close, attraction as tending to the equilibrium, asymptotic stability as both, exponential stability with a rate and local constants. Derive a one-dimensional map perturbation `δ_next≈F'(x*)δ` and a flow perturbation `δ'=f'(x*)δ`. Explain why modulus<1 is the discrete test and negative real part is the continuous test. Strict tests are sufficient; equality is inconclusive. Compare `x'=−x³`, `x'=x³` and `x'=0` at zero. The first decays algebraically, the second repels and the third is stable without attraction.
3. **Follow a phase line and find its basin.** Use `x'=a x−x³`. For a=1, equilibria−1,0,1 and sign arrows reveal the two basins and unstable separator. Derive `V=x⁴/4−a x²/2` and `V'=−(a x−x³)²`; shifting V around a selected minimum is needed for a local positive-definite certificate. A decreasing potential can converge to different critical points, not necessarily one global optimum. Continue a through0 to expose the pitchfork; at exactly a=0 use the nonlinear cubic. A deeper tilted potential `x'=b+x−x³` has folds at x=±1/√3, b=∓2/(3√3), explaining hysteresis under a declared slow equilibrium-following idealization. Dynamic rate-dependent lag is a different question.
4. **Read time and geometry together.** Explain velocity arrows, fixed points, trajectories, units and projection. Linear center `q'=−p,p'=q` has closed orbits but no attracting limit cycle. Compare a stable spiral and saddle; a stable saddle direction does not make the equilibrium stable. A nonnormal triangular system `q'=−q+6p,p'=−2p` has exact q(t)=6(e⁻ᵗ−e⁻²ᵗ),p(t)=e⁻²ᵗ from(0,1), hence transient growth despite negative eigenvalues. This qualifies “every step shrinks” without denying asymptotic stability.
5. **How is a stable oscillation born?** Work the Cartesian normal form `q'=a q−p−(q²+p²)q`, `p'=q+a p−(q²+p²)p`. Derive `r'=a r−r³, θ'=1`; for a>0 the radius√a attracts every nonzero initial radius, while phase need not synchronize. Solve the squared-radius logistic equation and handle a=0 separately. Define an attracting limit cycle as a set, distinguish orbital from same-phase convergence, and derive its radial return multiplier exp(−4πa). Explain a transverse Poincaré section; a flow's phase direction has a neutral multiplier. State the compact, invariant, no-equilibrium planar condition for Poincaré–Bendixson rather than claiming all two-dimensional-looking plots cannot be chaotic. Forced systems gain a clock state; maps are different.
6. **Repeat a map and watch its multiplier change.** Explain the logistic rule's dimensionless population interpretation and `0≤r≤4` invariant interval. Cobweb steps alternate map evaluation and carrying the result to the next input via y=x. Derive both fixed points and derivative2−r at the nonzero one; exact equality cases are not certified by the strict test. Factor F(F(x))−x, identify the genuine two-cycle points and multiplier4+2r−r². It attracts for3<r<1+√6. Show the finite sampled bifurcation diagram with burn-in, seed, iterations and parameter resolution explicitly stated; r≈3.56995 is the accumulation value, not a promise that every larger r is chaotic. Include a periodic window.
7. **Measure sensitivity without confusing three quantities.** Preserve the old two-trajectory program, then use a noncritical initial state in the main investigation. Distinguish actual finite separation, tangent propagation along one reference orbit, and a finite-time average of log|F'|. Derive the product by the chain rule and its log sum. A finite perturbation leaves the linear regime and separation saturates on a bounded interval; do not fit its final saturation as a Lyapunov rate. Zero derivative is a real log singularity, not an epsilon to erase. A positive exponent at the exact unstable fixed point x=.75,r=4 coexists with a period-one reference orbit: it is not sufficient proof that that orbit is chaotic. Explain the approximate forecast horizon log(tolerance/initial_error)/λ only within a valid small-error growth model.
8. **Why can stretching coexist with bounded motion?** The full tent map stretches each half by2 and folds it back. Derive `h(y)=sin²(πy/2)` and `4h(y)(1−h(y))=h(T(y))`. Explain branch intervals, their2⁻ⁿ widths and how each maps across the whole interval; use inverse-branch contractions to construct dense periodic points and explain mixing/sensitivity. This is a mathematical mechanism, distinct from the sampled browser trajectories. Uniform tent measure transforms to density `1/[π√(x(1−x))]`; derive normalization and mean1/2. State the measure/almost-everywhere conditions for exponent log2, and retain exceptional periodic points. Finite deterministic arithmetic has finitely many states and eventually repeats; a quantized tent example exposes why a computer orbit cannot itself prove infinite aperiodicity.
9. **Interpret a higher-dimensional model.** Introduce the Lorenz state and parameters as a reduced, dimensionless deterministic model, not weather measurements. Derive equilibria at zero and `(±√[β(ρ−1)],±√[β(ρ−1)],ρ−1)` whenρ>1. The Jacobian divergence is−σ−1−β: it contracts volumes but does not itself establish boundedness, an attractor or chaos. A declared finite RK4 calculation with σ=10,ρ=28,β=8/3 shows linked projections from one trajectory and flags crossings as projections. Check short-time results against an independently controlled solver, vary steps, and distinguish finite accuracy evidence from a long-time theorem. Optional volume/absorbing-region reasoning earns a separate derivation if included.
10. **Separate physical evolution from numerical evolution.** Cooling Euler has multiplier1−kh, stable for0<kh<2 but accurate only with an error check. For `q'=p,p'=−q`, derive exact energy conservation, forward Euler energy factor1+h² per step, and kick-then-drift symplectic Euler. The latter preserves area and a modified quadratic `q²+p²−h qp`, positive definite for|h|<2; it does not preserve the exact oscillator energy at every step. Compare same physical horizon, step, initial conditions and units. Teach solve_ivp tolerance and event contracts without claiming local tolerances are a global error guarantee or that arbitrary missing events are found.
11. **Apply and practise.** Connect loss decrease along gradient flow to repeated optimization updates, and products of state-dependent Jacobians to recurrence/rollout errors. Supply a changed-input capstone that compares a model's equilibrium/multiplier, a finite trace, numerical refinement and what can actually be concluded. Link the actual next Itô topic as the extension that adds random increments, plus annotated deeper learning resources.
