## Mathematical and computational contracts

- Finite plans use nonnegative weights of equal total; probability mode sums to1. Zero-weight rows/columns can be removed and restored. Finite costs and positive active weights give a unique positive entropic plan; forbidden edges need separate support-feasibility analysis.
- Generic transport cost need not be a metric. W_r uses d^r with r≥1 and the final rth root; general probability laws need finite rth moments. Explain metric properties using a finite glued coupling and Minkowski rather than claiming every arbitrary cost works.
- A Monge map sends one source atom to one destination; a Kantorovich coupling can split it. Equal-size uniform assignment admits a permutation optimum but does not imply uniqueness or that every optimal coupling is a map.
- The finite dual follows the Lagrangian and weak-gap identity; equality certifies optimum. General existence/Brenier statements retain their stated assumptions (Euclidean quadratic cost, finite second moments, source absolutely continuous).
- For the entropy convention used in code, Fε may be negative and self-comparison need not be zero. The plain linear term is another value. KL(π||a⊗b) differs by marginal-only constants; those cancel from the debiased combination when each solve uses the same convention.
- Sinkhorn divergence's zero self-value is algebraic; positivity/separation require suitable costs/kernels. State a supported Euclidean bounded-support setting and avoid declaring a metric or an unbiased population estimate.
- Report both marginal residual and iteration cap. Row/column normalization changes the other marginal; tiny residual alone does not certify zero regularization bias or population accuracy. Do not label an infeasible intermediate objective as a primal upper bound.
- Log-domain updates use stable maximum-shifted logsumexp and explicit finite bounds. They prevent naive exponential failure but do not eliminate ill-conditioning, finite iteration error or the need to audit restored probabilities.
- Dense cost/storage costs O(nm), one matrix-scaling sweep O(nm); total depends on requested error, costs, ε and implementation. No invented benchmark, universal threshold or speed claim. Sorting/1D, blocked matrix operations, lazy kernels and selected projections solve different performance needs without eager browser dependencies.
- Barycentric projection is a conditional average and generally does not push a discrete source to the target. A displacement interpolation built from a coupling does retain its endpoints. Uniform quantile interpolation gives a natural 1D W2 example; ordinary mixtures give a different path.

Native programs will include original scaling, exact two-by-two solve/certificate, sorted weighted transport/CDF, stable log-domain scaling with residuals, three objective quantities, barycentric/interpolation and a changed application/extension where useful. Use Python standard library or SciPy with explicit setup; execute every exact output and verify against independent LP, quadrature/Fraction or optimization.

Practice changes supplies/demands, ground geometry, Wasserstein order, feasibility/certificate, CDF gaps, ε/cost scaling, self-comparison convention, projection and missing-mass assumptions.
