## Reading route and mathematical contracts

1. Choose a state, information history and clock. Explain additive drift/noise increment laws and units before differential notation. Distinguish process noise from uncertainty about an unknown parameter.
2. Turn Brownian increments into an integral. Use a small finite sequence first. The exact identities for any grid are left sum=(W_T squared−W_0 squared−Q)/2, right sum=(W_T squared−W_0 squared+Q)/2, and symmetric endpoint sum=(W_T squared−W_0 squared)/2. The first becomes the Itô example; the symmetric endpoint definition introduces the relevant Stratonovich limit. Avoid the ambiguous instruction to take a midpoint without saying which values are averaged.
3. Derive the chain and product rules. Show why squared stochastic increments accumulate, define the function derivatives and include a time-dependent worked function. State scalar C1,2 assumptions locally. Extend to a small correlated vector example only after explaining covariance and Hessian entries. A nonsmooth absolute-value transformation needs local time; mention this boundary without incorrectly applying the smooth rule.
4. Solve GBM end to end. Retain the original output; derive the log drift, exact positivity and population summaries. A selected additional fact is that almost every path can decay while its mean grows: choose coefficients with mu−sigma-squared/2 below zero but mu above zero and explain the role of rare large outcomes and lack of uniform integrability. Use the theorem W_t/t tends to zero with its almost-sure scope; a finite plot does not prove it.
5. Solve OU and connect paths with distributions. Derive the transition through the weighted Brownian integral. For theta positive, stationary variance is eta-squared/(2theta); a deterministic starting state approaches it but is not stationary at time zero. Include theta=0 as an explicitly handled additive Brownian limit. Mean reversion's positive restoring coefficient is part of the contract, not an automatic label for every sign.
6. Specify convention and existence before copying a solver. For dX=a_S dt+b circle-dW, the Itô drift is a_S+half b times b-prime in one dimension, under the stated smoothness. Interpret the conversion through a complete exponential example. Describe strong/weak solutions independently from strong/weak numerical error, and give a concrete local-Lipschitz explosion case. A general existence theorem is a stated theorem with a source and proof idea, not falsely proved by two simulations.
7. Implement EM and scalar Milstein. Explain each update contribution, all required inputs and terminal time. Show a negative EM value for a positive exact GBM process, without clamping or secretly substituting a log method. The scalar Milstein correction is not a universal multidimensional formula; noncommuting noise can require iterated stochastic integrals.
8. Couple grids and separate errors. GBM exact evaluation uses the same cumulative Brownian increments as the approximations. For OU, a transition drawn with the correct marginal normal variance is an exact skeleton in law; it is not automatically the exact same-noise path when compared with EM. If that comparison is offered, generate the joint weighted integral and raw Brownian increment with their actual covariance. Do not merely reuse the same standard-normal number and claim an exact strong coupling.
9. Establish convergence and stability boundaries. State global Lipschitz/growth and time/smoothness assumptions behind each selected order theorem; weak order needs its declared test function. Use stable finite analytic GBM errors alongside a sampled estimate, rather than a fitted straight line presented as proof. Derive the scalar mean-square multiplier for EM and contrast almost-sure versus second-moment stability. Separate step error, finite-sample error, finite-precision effects and model mismatch.
10. Derive generator/moment and forward/backward equations. For smooth densities and appropriate decay or boundary conditions, forward density derivatives act on coefficient times density. Backward expectations use the generator on the test function. A simple polynomial terminal-value example provides a fully checkable no-discount Feynman-Kac bridge; this is not a complete PDE boundary-theory lesson. The deeper constant Gaussian exponential tilt supplies one transparent change-of-measure example, with the true-martingale qualification visible before any broader Girsanov mention.
11. Model checks, independent practice and next steps. Revisit irregular time intervals, units, initial law, observation noise and retained state; explain that exact sampled values still miss continuous-time barrier events. Link the completed Stochastic33 constant-coefficient bridge result with its exact assumptions; do not extend it silently to state-dependent diffusion. Close with the actual next topic Numerical Methods and useful domain owners.
