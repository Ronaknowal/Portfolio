## Scope and ownership

| Need or discovery | Treatment and reason |
| --- | --- |
| Observing versus forcing | Replace the treatment mechanism explicitly, derive the truncated factorization under an acyclic causal model with independent exogenous noise when all relevant causes are included. Hidden causes remain explicit in finite fixtures. |
| Path reasoning | Teach forks, chains, colliders and conditioned descendants, then general d-separation. Active paths permit dependence; they do not guarantee it without additional assumptions. A graph is a causal assumption, not automatically a discovered truth. |
| Adjustment and overlap | Derive the population-weighted backdoor expression and exchangeability/consistency/positivity argument. Compare original counts, population model probabilities and sampling uncertainty without conflating them. |
| Unknown graph or latent mechanism | Exhibit two fully specified causal models with the same observed distribution and different interventions. Identification is uniqueness across compatible models; more data cannot resolve an observational equivalence by itself. |
| All three do-calculus rules | State disjoint sets, modified graphs, conditional support, and rule 3's ancestor exclusion precisely. Build valid/invalid examples and an explicit frontdoor derivation. Do not turn a failed hand attempt into proof of nonidentification or claim a small lab implements complete ID. |
| Frontdoor | State all three criteria, calculate a hidden-confounding example, and change an exclusion assumption so the naive frontdoor formula fails. No claim that adjusting for any mediator works. |
| Counterfactuals and potential outcomes | Use common exogenous types, abduction/action/prediction and paired outcomes. Two couplings have the same randomized interventional marginals but different conditional individual counterfactuals; do not confuse potential outcomes with observed labels or claim all counterfactuals follow from a DAG alone. |
| Practical estimation | Derive standardization, inverse-propensity weighting and an augmented residual estimator in the finite model. Explain population double robustness and its limits; nuisance fitting, cross-fitting, overlap and uncertainty are not skipped by using a sophisticated learner. |
| Experiments and instruments | Distinguish assignment effect from treatment effect. Give a complete binary compliance/IV example with relevance, random assignment, exclusion, no interference/consistency and monotonicity, showing complier effect can differ from population ATE. Compare RDD/DiD briefly with their defining assumptions rather than pretending one estimator identifies every question. |
| Richer causal work | Full longitudinal treatment regimes, transportability, path-specific/counterfactual identification algorithms and semiparametric inference are further branches requiring more design; this core develops their prerequisite distinctions, not a claim of exhaustive mastery. Causal discovery/BN inference belongs with Bayesian Networks; intervention specificity/common-input interpretation has a later Neural Inference owner. |

## Concept sequence and evidence opportunities

1. State an offer/intervention question and distinguish population, outcome, exposure/treatment, time horizon and association.
2. Build acyclic mechanisms, perform intervention surgery and contrast it with selecting naturally observed cases; introduce local conditional factors and hidden causes.
3. Trace paths and conditioning, including collider descendants; use a small exact selection example rather than a verbal warning alone.
4. Derive backdoor standardization and potential-outcome assumptions, execute the preserved counts, change allocation/prevalence and diagnose missing overlap.
5. Prove why a query can remain unidentified with the same observed data under two compatible structural explanations.
6. Read the graph operations behind all three rules, validate a transformation, and explain the ancestor exception when conditioning on a downstream variable.
7. Derive and calculate frontdoor identification with hidden confounding; add a direct path and compare the incorrect formula with actual intervention.
8. Compute an individual counterfactual using the same hidden unit, then vary the unidentifiable coupling while retaining the same observed/experimental marginals.
9. Estimate an identified target: weighting/residual corrections, population versus finite-sample guarantees, diagnostics and uncertainty.
10. Contrast randomized assignment, treatment receipt and complier effects, and map the assumptions of alternative designs.
11. Solve changed diagrams, distributions, rules, counterfactual evidence and estimation errors with separate hints and complete solutions. Finish with an analysis plan/capstone and a clear next-route bridge.

Essential graph conditions and algebra remain in the first-pass route. Optional disclosures hold longer derivations and advanced estimation/design comparisons, not missing prerequisites.
