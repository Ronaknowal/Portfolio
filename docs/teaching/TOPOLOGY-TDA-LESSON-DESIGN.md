## Learning route and mathematical contracts

### 1. Decide which changes should count as the same shape

Start with a circle, an ellipse, a line segment and a filled disk drawn as different objects. The map `(x,y)→(x,ay)` is a homeomorphism of the plane for `a>0`; its inverse divides the second coordinate by a. At a=0 it identifies different points. Distances and persistence scales can change even before that collapse. A disk contracts to a point through `H(x,t)=(1−t)x`, but is not homeomorphic to a point. A circle cannot contract *within itself*; allowing the surrounding disk changes the space. A map between two spaces and a deformation of maps must not be conflated.

Introduce metric balls and open neighborhoods through an actual point on a circle and an interval endpoint. Relative openness differs from openness in the surrounding plane. Define the topology axioms, closed complements, interior, closure and boundary `closure(A)\interior(A)`, with an open interval and a singleton example. Open and closed are not mutually exclusive. Define continuous inverse images and connect with a local distance test. Distinguish connected and path-connected in general; finite complexes used here are locally path-connected. A compact finite complex and continuous real function supply a bounded range; “closed and bounded” is a Euclidean criterion, not a universal definition. A short optional note contrasts topology with the lengths/angles/smooth structure of geometry.

The intuition must not overtake the exact statement. Homeomorphisms preserve topology; homotopy equivalences preserve homology but need not be homeomorphisms; equal Betti numbers do not classify spaces. Present the disk/point and circle/ellipse examples before a comparison table.

### 2. Build the object before counting its features

Define abstract simplices as finite vertex sets closed under nonempty subsets. Distinguish a three-edge triangle from its filled2-simplex. A drawn crossing is not a vertex unless declared, and overlapping triangle drawings may be a projection of an abstract complex rather than a planar geometric complex.

Keep the Rips convention `diameter≤epsilon` throughout. Each simplex enters when its largest pairwise distance is reached; vertices enter at0. An epsilon control changes the **complex**, not the raw observations. Equal distances enter at the same actual scale, regardless of implementation tie order. Scaling all coordinates by c>0 scales finite birth/death values by c; anisotropic scaling generally changes them nonuniformly.

Compare with the Čech nerve of closed Euclidean balls of radius `epsilon/2`. Three vertices of an equilateral triangle of side2 have all edges at epsilon2. Rips also inserts the face then, while a common three-ball intersection first appears at epsilon`4/√3`. Derive this from the center-to-vertex distance. Pairwise intersection is not common intersection. The nerve theorem requires the relevant contractible intersections; state the finite convex-ball setting rather than promising arbitrary sensor coverage. Introduce alpha complexes as nerves of ball∩Voronoi regions. Do not define every alpha face by its own circumradius alone: an obtuse coface can determine when a face enters. Distinguish radius from squared-radius library conventions if software is shown.

### 3. Turn cancellation into an interpretable count

A k-chain is a list of k-simplices with coefficients0/1; adding chains is symmetric difference. Two copies cancel because `1+1=0` in F2. The boundary of an edge is its two endpoints; the boundary of a triangle is its three edges. Draw each appearance of a vertex when taking a triangle boundary twice and pair cancellations, proving `∂²=0`.

Define cycle as zero boundary, boundary as the boundary of a higher-dimensional chain, and homology class as cycles that differ by a boundary. Work the same three-edge cycle before and after filling. Explain `Hk=ker∂k/im∂(k+1)` and derive `beta_k=n_k−rank∂k−rank∂(k+1)` over the declared field. Give the nullspace/rank meaning before matrix notation. The graph formula `beta1=E−V+components` holds when no faces have been added; with faces their boundary rank must be subtracted. Euler characteristic checks the alternating sum, but is not a substitute for all Betti numbers.

A tetrahedral shell has a2-cycle of four faces and no3-cell filling; filling the tetrahedron removes it. This separates void from graph loop and explains why computing Hk requires cells through dimension k+1. A2-skeleton of a complete four-vertex complex has artificial H2 if the intended full Rips complex should include a3-simplex. Browser point-cloud output is explicitly H0/H1 with dimension2; the separate shell example includes a3-cell when selected. Introduce field dependence via a precise small chain-matrix example, with a sourced topological interpretation only if fully explained; no arbitrary real-valued rank routine on F2 data.

### 4. Follow what survives, not just the number at each snapshot

A filtration is a nested family with inclusion maps. Applying homology tracks which earlier classes remain non-boundaries later. Equal counts at two scales do not establish that the same class survived. Use a finite sequence where one cycle is filled while another appears to show the distinction.

Work square corners `(−1,−1),(1,−1),(1,1),(−1,1)`. For epsilon<2 there are four components. At2 the four perimeter edges connect them and create one H1 class. At`2√2` diagonals and faces remove the H1 class. The positive barcode has three finite H0 bars[0,2), one essential H0 bar[0,∞), and H1[2,2√2). All tie-created zero-length pairs are bookkeeping events, not additional positive-duration features. Explain half-open bars and equality at death.

Place barcode, current complex, and diagram together, with one selected bar identifying birth/death and a representative when meaningful. Representatives are not unique or guaranteed minimal. Tie ordering can change representatives/zero pairs while leaving positive persistence unchanged. Essential∞ means surviving the defined full filtration; a computation stopped at a cutoff only establishes “not yet died” unless the continuation is known. Diagonal multiplicity matters for matching, not a cloud of infinitely many rendered points.

### 5. Compute a whole finite filtration

Preserve the original connectivity program in its explanatory context, then connect H0 deaths to the accepted edge weights of a minimum spanning tree/forest with the vertex birth convention explicit. Explain how single linkage can chain through a bridge point.

Give a complete original F2 matrix-reduction program. Sort simplices by filtration value, then dimension, then deterministic vertex order so faces precede cofaces. A boundary column records the exact faces. Reduce columns left-to-right: when the greatest nonzero row is already a pivot, XOR the earlier reduced column to cancel it. Each cancellation strictly lowers that pivot. Explain the invariant: only earlier columns of the same dimension are added, so the filtration-respecting boundary span is unchanged. A zero reduced column creates a class; a nonzero pivot pairs a prior creator with this destructor. Track column combinations for the small visual trace; do not infer a geometric representative from a bare pivot alone.

Trace enough of a filled triangle and the square to expose an actual cancellation, not only the final diagram. Prove termination and explain the birth/death pairing through the invariant; clearly label any general pairing theorem that is cited rather than fully reproved. The straightforward worst case is O(S³) elementary field operations and O(S²) storage for S simplices. Sparse storage can help but is not a universal linear-time guarantee. Rips has combinatorial growth: limiting dimension is a deliberate output contract, and subsampling changes the analyzed object. Use bounded browser inputs and separate production library guidance.

### 6. Compare diagrams and say exactly what stability means

Start with one bar[1,4) and one[1.2,3.8). Pair cost in L∞ is.2. Sending the first point to the diagonal costs1.5, attained at(2.5,2.5). Define partial matching of finite off-diagonal multisets, unmatched-to-diagonal cost, bottleneck maximum and minimum over matchings. Include multiplicities, unmatched points in either diagram and a small changed matching where greedy nearest-neighbor assignment fails. Infinite bars require a separate compatible treatment; the bounded matching lab uses finite diagrams only and says so.

Define the p-Wasserstein sum and state the chosen ground norm. Do not confuse this with transport between normalized point-cloud probability measures: finite diagram cardinality need not match and the diagonal absorbs unmatched features.

For two finite filtrations on the same complex with filtration values differing by at most eta, show the eta-shift inclusions and their composition. State the bottleneck stability theorem with its finite/tame assumptions; the correspondence to a matching is nontrivial, not proved merely by two pictures. For paired points moving at most delta in Euclidean distance, triangle inequality bounds every pair-distance change by2delta, giving the corresponding Rips comparison. State the paired-input and same-convention hypotheses. A tiny *fraction* of arbitrary distant outliers is not necessarily a tiny perturbation in this metric. Large persistence is not a p-value, causal claim, coverage certificate or universal predictive importance.

### 7. Let the data representation change the construction

Use a3×3 grayscale toy image. Each pixel is a closed square entering at its scalar value; all boundary edges/vertices enter with the minimum incident pixel value. Low-valued ring pixels and a high-valued center produce a finite H1 interval; opening a gap or filling the center changes it for an explicit reason. State that squares meeting at a corner share a vertex and are connected under this cubical convention. This is not interchangeable with a4-neighbor foreground convention. A small planar union can be checked independently using components and Euler characteristic, or by consistent triangulation.

Explain sublevel versus superlevel choice, scalar units, foreground/background and boundary conditions. Periodic edge identification changes the domain to a different space; it is not an innocuous plotting option. Retain point-cloud/image/graph/time-series alternatives without pretending one Rips construction fits every input.

An interesting exact connection uses `s(t)=cos(t)` and delay vector `(s(t),s(t−tau))`. For tau=π/2 the trace is a circle; for nonzero sin(tau) it is an ellipse; tau=0 orπ collapses to a segment. Derive its2×2 linear map and determinant. This shows why delay choice can expose or erase a cycle; finite sampling/noise still matter. It is a synthetic periodic-signal example, not proof of the general Takens theorem or a biological oscillator diagnosis. A short native computation should preserve time order for constructing windows while explaining that ordinary point-cloud persistence then discards ordering/direction.

### 8. Turn features into another measurable object

Define a landscape from the tent `max(0,min(t−b,d−t))` for each finite bar and the kth-largest tent height at each t. Work overlapping bars with a crossing and explain why one maximum curve discards information. Sampled grid vectors are approximations to full landscapes. Connect back to the previous function-space lesson: averaging functions becomes available, with its own integrability conditions.

For a persistence image, map(b,d) to(b,d−b), multiply a normalized Gaussian by a stated continuous weight that vanishes at zero persistence, then **integrate over pixels**. Derive the separable normal-CDF rectangle formula. Pixel-center evaluations are not the same operation. Show the fixed grid, bandwidth, weights, finite window and excluded infinite bars. Pixel values are weighted feature contributions, not a posterior probability that a hole exists. A finite window loses Gaussian tail mass; do not renormalize silently.

Provide a complete small synthetic circle-versus-filled-region experiment with matched sample counts and a declared seed. Use training data to set any scaler/grid or descriptor parameters; choose variants on validation data and evaluate untouched test data once. Include a simple geometric or distance baseline and report actual results without preselecting a winning method. Add an anisotropic/noise/uneven-sampling diagnostic so the original practice question gains an explained answer. Distinguish resampling a single correlated trajectory from independent experimental units. The experiment illustrates a workflow, not a general performance benchmark.

### 9. Summarize overlap with Mapper

Introduce a scalar lens on an actual small point set, an overlapping cover of its value range, clustering inside each pulled-back interval, and the nerve of the resulting point-ID sets. A source observation can belong to several nodes; an edge represents shared observations, not just geometric closeness of graph-node drawings. Compute a complete small example and change cover overlap or within-band clustering to see why the result changes. Show the actual members beside graph nodes. Returning the1-skeleton is a stated display choice and omits higher common intersections.

Connect to Reeb graphs by describing connected components of exact level sets, then explain that point-cloud clustering/finite cover is an approximation with assumptions. A good cover of lens values alone does not imply the pulled-back clusters form a good cover of the original space. Mapper output is not automatically homotopy-equivalent to the data-generating shape. The diagram is exploratory evidence requiring parameter/sampling checks.

### 10. Assess claims, practise transfer and choose the next question

Conclude with a worked evidence report: object and unit, representation/metric, filtration and scale convention, dimension/field/cutoff, mathematical result, data sensitivity, baseline/split and remaining uncertainty. Define core readiness by changed calculations and diagnosis, not the number of plots.

Optional branches distinguish homology from fundamental group/cohomology, topological from geometric equivalence, ordinary from extended/zigzag/multiparameter persistence, and summary stability from statistical inference. Explain the reason each branch exists with one concrete limitation, then link primary study. Do not claim a single interval barcode classifies a general multiparameter module. The next Category Theory lesson can organize the inclusion→linear-map structure already encountered here.
