## Learning route and actual hurdles

1. **What is combined?** Start with forecasts of the same shipment outcome, in hours, and two classifiers predicting the same declared event. Separate raw features, saved fitted models, base predictions, combining rule and eventual target. Establish reserved evaluation before choosing a combination. A small immediately visible prediction ledger exposes the data unit; no history-first opening.
2. **Votes and averages answer different questions.** Calculate weighted regression averages, hard ballots and class-aligned probability means. Explain nonnegative normalized weights, tie policies, absent/misaligned classes and why scores or model parameters cannot be silently averaged as probabilities. The same concrete case can change class under hard versus soft voting. Combining three copies changes neither an ordinary mean nor the amount of evidence. Explain finite independent-majority improvement and its dependence assumption, with a copied-error counterexample.
3. **How do errors combine?** Connect signed errors to the weighted mean visually. Expand weighted squared losses into ensemble loss plus disagreement. This guarantees no worse than the weighted average loss under its assumptions, not no worse than the best member. Derive the two-member least-squares weight, its denominator-zero case and clipping for a convex blend. Distinguish fitting weights from evaluating them. Refresh covariance with fixed-query randomness; retain the equal-variance formula and its limits without duplicating Trees' slider.
4. **What does bootstrap training actually change?** Draw named rows with replacement, preserve multiplicity, fit an actual weighted two-leaf regression rule and inspect the predictions. OOB eligibility belongs to each row/model pair. Derive missing-row probability `(1−1/n)^m`, expected represented rows and no-OOB-vote risk. An OOB estimate has assumptions and selection limitations; it is not an independent test set or free certainty. Contrast bootstrap with sampling without replacement and randomized feature choices.
5. **Boosting changes the next training objective.** Use six explicit ±1-labeled points and threshold/polarity stumps. A weighted mistake is a sum of masses, not a count or a model's vote weight. Fit a stump, calculate epsilon and alpha, update normalized row masses, and add signed votes. Preserve the full tree/stump circles example as a deeper executable comparison; explain observed results without inventing which samples are irreducibly noisy.
6. **Why that alpha?** Group correct and incorrect weighted rows into `(1−epsilon)e^(−alpha)+epsilon e^alpha`; take the derivative, find the minimizer for `0<epsilon<1`, and substitute the normalizer. Explain zero error, no positive edge and vote ties with explicit implementation conventions. Multiply round normalizers, bound training mistakes by exponential loss, then derive `exp(−2 sum gamma_t²)` and the additional uniform-edge hypothesis. Shrinkage, margins and noisy labels receive precise limits. Current SAMME binary normalized weights agree with signed AdaBoost for nondegenerate rounds even though stored vote weights are twice as large; no claim of identical probabilities or all boundary behavior.
7. **Stacking is a second fitted problem.** Build a six-row, two-column OOF matrix one fold at a time, with exact row IDs and training ownership. Contrast in-sample nearest-neighbor memorization with genuine OOF predictions. Fit the combining weight on OOF residuals, then apply the saved weight to fully refitted bases at a new query. State the resulting fold-fit/full-fit distribution mismatch. The meta-model may use the target; the base prediction supplying that row must respect its holdout boundary.
8. **Actual classifier stacks and protocol choices.** Work through probability/decision-score/label shapes and `stack_method='auto'`; binary probability complements, multiclass columns, `passthrough` and consistent preprocessing. Put preprocessing inside each base pipeline. Explain why `transform(X_train)` after fitting returns full-fit predictions, not the saved OOF matrix. Blending uses a disjoint combiner set and has a distinct data tradeoff. `cv='prefit'` is valid only with appropriate prior fit ownership. Integer CV is prediction generation, not stack evaluation.
9. **Groups and time change legal rows.** An immediately visible fold-membership diagram shows future leakage and duplicate entity leakage. Supply a complete forward-only OOF loop with an uncovered initial prefix; fit the meta-model only on covered rows. Current `StackingClassifier` delegates to `cross_val_predict`, whose partition requirement makes naïvely passing TimeSeriesSplit fail. Group handling uses explicit group-safe split indices to avoid assuming undocumented automatic metadata routing.
10. **Learn from disagreement without trusting it blindly.** An actual computed held-out 2D prediction view lets the reader compare individual and combined decisions, inspect a selected query and see where predictions agree or conflict. Scores come from the displayed native fitted procedure, not illustrative model rankings. A finite exact law proves calibrated members need not produce a calibrated mean; correct probability use and cost-sensitive decisions remain separate from accuracy. Unanimous wrong predictions are a failure, not uncertainty evidence.
11. **Complete controlled experiment and serving decision.** Native training/validation/test data and seeds are explicit. Fit bases and stack only inside training ownership, choose a limited combination using validation, then report the frozen candidate and strongest single baseline on test. Report accuracy, a proper probability score and explicit decision cost where appropriate. Include a model-removal comparison selected without test tuning. Explain training fit count, serial/parallel dependency path, memory/latency measurement and why a small empirical gain may not pay its cost. Hypothetical timings are clearly inputs to arithmetic, not benchmarks.
12. **When context must change the weights.** A compact deeper FWLS construction explains cross-features `context × base prediction`. Derive why adding a context column to a linear combiner only shifts an intercept, whereas product columns change slopes. Work a held-out toy regression protocol, rank/redundancy and ridge choices, limitations of negative/non-normalized weights, and an independently changed example. This is a concrete bridge to recommendation contexts; no Netflix performance claim is needed to teach it.
13. **Independent practice and next step.** Problems assess all the mechanisms above, and a complete changed capstone report provides a checkable acceptable result plus reasoning. Link the next Recommender Systems entry in the actual module order. References offer alternate explanations and current API details; they do not replace local teaching.
