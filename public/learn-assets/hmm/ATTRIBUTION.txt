ewt-sequences.json is the extract this lesson serves: 89597 bytes,
SHA-256 e801e665c4e2a6fa002e04ebb52b00b4fbc1421c7f029494565c0ecbaa016a4d.
It is a JSON list of 200 records, each holding a split name, the original EWT sentence id, the
sentence's tokens and their original UPOS tags. Extraction kept integer-id syntactic word rows and
excluded multiword range rows and decimal empty nodes. It took the first 120, 40 and 40 sentences of
length 3 to 15 from the official r2.16 train, dev and test files, giving 1188, 341 and 370 tokens.
All 200 sentence ids are distinct. Selecting short sentences first is deliberately biased: this is an
instructional extract, not a representative benchmark sample.

What this lesson does with it, and does not do to it: the served file is unchanged, and every
original UPOS tag is preserved in it. The lesson additionally defines a deliberately coarse
three-way teaching task - NOUN and PROPN become Noun, VERB and AUX become Verb, and every other
original tag becomes Other - and fits a supervised categorical HMM by counting on the 120 training
sentences only. That coarse mapping is lossy and is a teaching simplification, not a linguistic
claim and not full part-of-speech tagging. Development words never change the vocabulary. The 40
reserved sentences are not scored anywhere in this lesson; educational reuse elsewhere does not make
them a fresh research holdout.
